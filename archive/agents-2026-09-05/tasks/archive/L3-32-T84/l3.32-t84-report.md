### 2.3 Defects 2 and 3, re-derived (my own index arithmetic)

From the satisfaction clause `γ ⊨ (∃̇∈ t φ) = ⋁ S (λ x → (x ∈ˢ ⟦ t ⟧ γ) ⊓ ((x ∷ γ) ⊨ φ))`
(`FOL/Semantics.lagda.md:103`) and the de Bruijn convention (each binder
prepends its witness, so the bound variable sits at index 0).

**Defect 2: `snd∈Snd` asserts equality of second components, the limit
clause needs membership.** At `(k, l)` (`Condensation.lagda.md:396-401`) the
formula binds `x₁ ∈ k` (pinned singleton by `sgl`), `y₂ ∈ k` (pinned
two-element by `pair2`), then `z ∈ y₂` with "every member of `x₁` differs
from `z`" and `sndIn z l`. With `k = pr a b`: `x₁ = {a}`, `y₂ = {a,b}`,
so "every member of x₁ differs from z" is `a ≠ z`, and `sndIn z l` (post-T76)
forces `z = snd(l)`; since `z ∈ {a,b}` and `z ≠ a`, `z = b = snd(k)`. The
formula therefore reads **`snd(k) = snd(l)`**, while the delivered limit
clause needs **`snd(k) ∈ snd(l)`**
(`limitClause`, `LevelFormula.lagda.md:152-155`). Machine-checked in my
probe against the real embedded formulas: the membership `one ∈ two` holds
(`snd(pr ∅ one) = one`, `snd(pr ∅ two) = two`,
`/private/tmp/T84Defects.agda`, `one∈two`), and T76's `snd∈Snd-sat` /
T77's `snd∈Snd-ref` (exit 0 at their time; the formulas are unchanged since)
show the equal-second case satisfies it and the unequal case is refuted.

**Defect 3: `limIn`'s second binder ranges over the singleton member.** At
`(p, f, x)` (`Condensation.lagda.md:503-510`) the first binder ranges over
members `x₁` of `p` (pinned singleton), and the second binder
`∃̇∈ (var zero)` ranges over **`x₁`** (the singleton), not the pair member of
`p`: with `p = pr a b`, `x₁ = {a}`, so the `pair2` witness is forced to be a
member of `{a}` (i.e. `a`), which is not a two-element set, and the rest of
the clause is vacuous. Machine-checked: the two possible members of the
singleton are refuted as two-element sets in my probe (`¬pair2-∅`,
`¬pair2-x`, `/private/tmp/T84Defects.agda`), and T76's `limIn-unsat` (exit 0
at the time) refutes the whole formula at `(pr ∅ one, f, x)` for every
`f x`. The intended reading is the delivered one: every member `z` of the
value `y` is the second component of a pair `pr b z ∈ f` with `b ∈ a`
(`LevelFormula.lagda.md:245-249`).

## 3. Defects 2 and 3 fixed, and fixed by construction?

**Not built: this dispatch stopped at the D-10 check (section 1).** The fix
is designed and its machinery is machine-verified, so the stop is priced
rather than open-ended.

The decode that makes each impossible to restate wrongly is the limit
clause's two-way decode against the delivered `limitClause`, in the shape the
kit's consumer pattern already delivers
(`LevelFormula.lagda.md:152-155` meta, `:245-262` object, `:267-374`
decodes). Its binder arithmetic, checked by hand:

- `limPr` at the arity-4 environment `(y, a, f, x)`: `∃̇∈ (var (suc (suc zero)))`
  binds `p ∈ f`, then `prAt zero (suc (suc zero)) (suc zero)` reads "`p` is
  the pair `pr a y`": the pair `(a, y)` lies in the graph.
- `limIn`: bind `z`; premise `z ∈ y`; then `∃̇∈ (var (suc (suc zero)))` binds
  `p ∈ a` and `∃̇∈ (var (suc (suc (suc (suc zero)))))` binds `q ∈ f`, with
  `prAt zero (suc zero) (suc (suc zero))` reading "`q` is the pair `pr p z`".
  The reading is `z ∈ y ⇒ ∃ p ∈ a, ∃ q ∈ f, q = pr p z`, exactly
  `limitClause`'s first inclusion.
- `limOut` reads the converse: `(∃ p ∈ a, ∃ q ∈ f, q = pr p z) ⇒ z ∈ y`,
  the second inclusion.

Defect 3 cannot be restated: the clause has **no binder over a singleton
member at all**; the value's members `z` are quantified directly (premise
`z ∈ y`), and the pair `(a, y)` is read via `limPr`'s membership in the
graph. Defect 2 cannot be restated: the membership reading is expressed by
the pair atom `prAt` (a decode against the pair characterization,
`PairAtoms.lagda.md`, `prAt-out`/`prAt-in`), and no second-component
equality atom exists anywhere. The decode statement's type IS the meaning: a
formula that did not read the union inclusion could not inhabit
`limit-out`'s statement, which is the delivered `limitClause` reading
itself. This is the C-NN discipline: the atom is not usable until its
two-way decode exists, and the decode's statement pins the reading.

The re-hosting machinery that would carry these decodes to the set carrier
is verified green (section 2.2, `/private/tmp/T84A.agda`): the transported
clause is the kit clause at the carrier (refl), and satisfaction at the
inner world connects to the kit's satisfaction (`⊨ᵐ-map`), so the kit's
`pair∈`/`isOrd-out`/limit decodes reach the embedded parameter-free story.

## 7. Walls by LESSONS class

**Wall class: D-10 / C-NN (a shape certificate is not a meaning certificate;
and now, its mirror: a clause kit's Levy profile is part of its interface).**
The wall is not a proof wall; it is a pricing wall found by the D-10 check
the brief demands. T79/T80 priced lever B on the assumption that the kit's
clause formulas could be consumed by a Levy-certified consumer; the kit was
built for the face's profile (unbounded, no Δ₀ certificates needed), and a
Δ₀-certifiable clause layer is real content wherever it lives (section 1's
routes (b) and (c)). The finding is machine-confirmed (the Δ₀ data has no
unbounded constructor; the kit's six clauses each contain one).

**Proposed as an extension of the proposed C-NN entry (which awaits the
owner's ID), not a new ID:** *a shared clause kit's Levy profile is part of
its interface, and it must be checked at kit-build time, not when the third
consumer arrives.* Measurement: the kit's clause layer (LevelFormula +
LevelSigma + the face) needed no Δ₀ witnesses, so the unbounded
quantification in its six clause formulas was invisible to every consumer
and every gate until the condensation consumer's obligations (`levelΔ₀`,
`level-abs₀`, the transfers) forced Δ₀; the check is the ten-line census of
unbounded constructors per clause formula (this report's section 2.1 table),
and the price of finding it late is the difference between the ruled lever
B arithmetic (+100 to +250) and the honest range (-260 to -410, section 1).
Enforcement point: when a parameterized clause kit is built or extended,
record whether its clause formulas are Δ₀-certifiable (bounded quantifiers)
beside the export table; a consumer that needs Levy certificates then pays
the bounded layer's price at kit time, not at fold time.

**No performance wall.** The probes check in 1-2 s warm; the machinery the
fold needs (transport refl, the connection lemma, the decode re-hosting) is
cheap. One conversion quirk was met in the probe (concrete-singleton
membership fibers do not reduce under `subst` at the use site, the R-35/I-4
class) and worked around by checking the vacuity mechanism at the refutation
level instead; it is a probe-level nuisance, not a wall of the build.

**The stop is the deliverable.** The tree is untouched, the finding is
machine-checked, and the re-price is itemized. What would unblock the row:
the owner either (i) funds the bounded clause layer (route (b), priced
~380-500 naive in the kit, with the chapter then shrinking by ~70-90), or
(ii) rules that the Δ₀ obligations may be re-hosted (the bespoke semantic
absoluteness per clause, priced at the clause-decode rate), or (iii) re-prices
lever B as an atom-level fold with the defect fixes and the clause decodes
