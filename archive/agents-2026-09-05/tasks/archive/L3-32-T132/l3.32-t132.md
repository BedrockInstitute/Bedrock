## THE SHAPE THE IDEAL FORM TAKES, AND WHY

`[T130]` established this and you should not re-derive it: the ideal form is
**not** a port of the twelve-clause encoding. It is `DefAt`'s semantics
**re-stated against the surviving `L.Definability`**, whose interface is:

```
smallSat : Formula ⟪ A ⟫ 1 → ⟪ A ⟫ → hProp ℓ          (src/L/Definability.lagda.md:107)
defSet   : Formula ⟪ A ⟫ 1 → S                        (:110-111)
```

with the two delivered specs: a `defSet φ` member does not leave `A`, and
`⟪ A ⟫↪ m ∈ defSet φ` **iff** the inner world satisfies `φ` at `m`
(`:129-132`).

`[T130]` also established the gap you must bridge, and it is the whole cost:
**`defSet` is META-level** (a `sett` in Agda, with the "syntax as index set"
trick making it cheap) while the consumer needs an **OBJECT-level** formula,
because `L.Condensation` reads one `Formula Sʟ 2` at two different
satisfactions (`AbsL.⊨ᵛ` and `AbsL.⊨ᵐ`, `src/L/Condensation.lagda.md:884-902`).
A meta-level `sett` has no satisfaction and no second reading. **The
syntax-as-index-set trick is unavailable inside an object formula**: the
object language's quantifiers range over sets, not over the host-level
`Formula ⟪ A ⟫ 1` index family.

So the object formula must say "b is the definable powerset of a" **in the
object language**, and its adequacy must connect that to `defSet`. That
connection is the 700 to 900 lines.
