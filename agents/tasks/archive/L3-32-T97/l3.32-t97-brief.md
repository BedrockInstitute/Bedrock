## WHY THIS DIG IS WORTH A DISPATCH

The retiring internalization subtree (`L.Coding`, `L.Godel`, `L.Choice`) checks
at **0.013 s per line and 0.074 s per obligation over 26,479 lines**. The
surviving trunk's three expensive theorems check at **2.234 s per obligation, a
30x gap**. `L.Godel.Closure` alone is 3,490 lines for 102 seconds, with **zero
`opaque`, zero `postulate`**.

Every recent attempt to transfer that cheapness has failed. `[T88]` measured
three fixes on `SquareLaw` and all three failed, one by exhausting the heap
twice. `[T89]` wrote the discipline into `Condensation` and the module did not
move at all. `[T87]`'s seal on `Bridge` worked and then plateaued at 14x.

**So the question is no longer "what does the cheap tree look like" (`[T93]`
answered that by reading it). The question is "HOW DID IT GET THAT WAY".**

Nobody has asked. And this repository contains the full history: if that route
ever had a slow phase that somebody fixed, the fix is in the git log, and it is
a proven in-repo solution to precisely the problem now blocking the campaign.

## THE THREE THINGS THAT WOULD EACH BE WORTH THE DISPATCH

1. **A slow phase that was cured.** Did `L.Godel.*` or `L.Coding.*` ever check
   slowly? Find the commit that fixed it and what it changed. A measured
   before-and-after inside this repository beats any amount of reasoning about
   what ought to work.
2. **A design decision taken up front for cost reasons.** If the route was
   built cheap by construction rather than made cheap later, the decision that
   made it so is the transferable asset. Find where it was taken and what was
   rejected.
3. **A wall that was hit and routed around.** If somebody tried a shape, found
   it expensive, and chose another, that negative result is exactly what
   `SquareLaw` needs now.

**If the honest answer is "it was never slow and nobody ever worked on its
speed", that is a real and valuable finding**, because it would mean the
cheapness follows from what that route PROVES rather than from how it was
written, which decides the campaign's central question. Say so plainly with the
evidence that shows it.
