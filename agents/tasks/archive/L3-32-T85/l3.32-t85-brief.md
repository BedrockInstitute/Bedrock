## WHY THE FIRST GATE DIED, AND WHAT THAT LEAVES STANDING

`[T59]`'s negative control landed green and near-free in 20 lines, which is
exactly what putting the cheapest arm first was for. The two upper arms died on
**two premises that separate reports had recorded as definitional**:

1. **`⟪ 𝒟ₒ A ⟫` is NOT definitionally `Formula ⟪ A ⟫ 1`.** In the installed
   cubical library, `⟪ sett X ix ⟫` is the quotient of `X` by `ix`-value
   equality, so the identification holds only up to that quotient. `[T5]` and
   `[T57]` had both recorded it as definitional.
2. **`Init ω` is UNINHABITED.** `Init α` requires `⟨ ω ∈ˢ α ⟩`
   (`src/L/Ordinal/SquareLaw.lagda.md:947-949`), so `Init ω` would need
   `ω ∈ ω`, refuted by `∈-irrefl` (`src/V/Hierarchy.lagda.md:155`).
   Consequently `SquareLaw.Initial ω` does not exist and
   `CardinalCount.Bound` at `β = ω` is uninstantiable, which is why both upper
   arms could not be built **at the level the gate had chosen**.

`[T62]` has since corrected the chapter's prose: the square law holds
**strictly above `ω`**, not from `ω` up. The theorem is unchanged and
non-vacuous.

**So the mathematics was never refuted. The gate picked an instance where its
ingredients do not exist.** That is a design fault, and it is yours to fix.

## THE HARD PART, WHICH IS THE REAL TASK

The first gate chose `ω` **because it is concrete and finite**, which is what
makes a probe cheap. But `Init` fails exactly there. The next initial ordinal
where `Init` holds is uncountable, and nothing about it is concrete.

**So: is there an instance that is both inhabited and cheap?** Candidates to
consider, and price or kill each:

- **A generic `Init α` with `α` a variable**, proving the successor and limit
  steps schematically rather than at a numeral. This trades concreteness for
  genericity and is the D29-shaped answer; say whether the steps can be written
  without a concrete carrier.
- **A weakened hypothesis** that is inhabited at `ω`: is there a predicate
  strictly weaker than `Init` that the successor step actually needs? `[T62]`
  reported that `Init` minus its membership clause IS inhabited at `ω` (the
  successor closure holds, the no-injection clause is vacuous, `IsOrd ω`
  holds), which is a strong hint.
- **Measuring only ONE step.** The gate does not have to be the whole
  induction. If the successor step alone can be built at a live instance, that
  measures the rate and moves the term, which is all D22 asks.
- **A different negative control**, since the first one succeeded and told us
  little.

**If no cheap inhabited instance exists, that is itself the deliverable**: say
so, and say what the term costs to fund unmeasured, so the owner rules with the
real choice in front of them.
