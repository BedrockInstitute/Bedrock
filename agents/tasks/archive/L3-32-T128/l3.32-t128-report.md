## 1. D-10 expressibility verdict (the gate's answer)

**EXPRESSIBLE.** The sixth clause is an object formula over the carrier in
the kit's language, with a two-way decode; the probe builds and checks all
three layers (the limit atom, the union reading, the clause assembly) at the
first limit where the clause has content. This is not the T84 obstruction.

**Why the union quantifier is not the brief's hazard.** The clause's union
reads `z ∈ b ⟷ ∃ ξ ∈ a, ∃ w, ∃ p ∈ f, p = pr ξ w ∧ z ∈ w`, and the
`ξ`-quantifier **is bounded inside the carrier**: the formula writes
`∃̇∈ (var a)` over members of the ordinal `a` (`src/ProbeT128.agda:121-125`),
and since the carrier `C` is transitive and `a ∈ C`, every `ξ ∈ a` is a
carrier member, so the bounded-quantifier machinery applies — the same
machinery every delivered kit decode consumes (`pair∈`,
`src/L/LevelKit.lagda.md:229`). The `w`-quantifier is unbounded in the
formula, but the satisfaction is read at the carrier-restricted structure
`𝒮ᵥ ↾ C`, whose universe is the carrier's members
(`src/FOL/ZFStructure.lagda.md:143-152`); and `w ∈ C` holds by transitivity
from `pr ξ w ∈ f ∈ C`. No witness escapes the carrier. The clause is not
Δ₀-certifiable (the `w` and `z` quantifiers are unbounded constructors), but
Δ₀ is the condensation consumer's requirement — that is exactly the T84
obstruction — and the below-lim consumer has no Levy obligation. The
brief's red trigger ("if that quantifier cannot be bounded inside the
carrier, the clause is not Delta-0") does not fire: the `ξ`-quantifier is
bounded, and the clause is green for this consumer for the same reason every
kit clause is green here — the kit's language is unbounded by design (all
six delivered clauses use `∃̇`/`∀̇`, `LevelKit.lagda.md:140-192`).

**R-35 and D-24 held.** The clause is stated pointwise, memberships at the
small index (`z ∈ b ⟷ ∃ ξ < a, z ∈ v ξ`), with no union representation built
or extracted — the R-35 cure, applied. It is not the D-24 trap in disguise:
the clause is a membership characterization with an honest bound (`ξ` ranges
over members of `a`), not a "block absorbs unbounded-but-finite offsets"
containment claim.

**The one genuinely new atom is the limit predicate, and it is expressible.**
"`a` is a limit ordinal" reads as: `a` is an ordinal (the kit's `isOrdAt`),
nonempty, and every member has a member above it (`limAt`,
`src/ProbeT128.agda:98-104`). The bridge to the delivered `isLimit`
(`IsOrd × ≠ ∅ × ¬succ`, `src/L/Rud/OrdArith.lagda.md:158-159`) is proved
both ways (`noAbove→succ` `:156`, `closedFromLim` `:175`): the not-a-
successor half is exactly "every member has a member above it", by ordinal
linearity (`ord-tri`, `src/L/Ordinal/Linear.lagda.md:136`) and
transitivity. The extraction direction is classical (`lem`, the standing
probe parameter).

## 2. The carrier, and why `ω` could not serve

Gated at `β₀ = +ω a₀` with `a₀ = +ω ∅` (`src/ProbeT128.agda:63-69`). At
`ω` the sixth clause has no content: every member of `ω` is a numeral

ordinal sits strictly below the carrier's index: `isLimit a₀` is
`+ω-limit ∅ ∅-ord` (`src/L/Rud/OrdBlocks.lagda.md:170`), and the content

OrdBlocks delivers both the first limit and the first-with-content carrier
in two lines each; no `ω·2` ordinal machinery needed to be built.
