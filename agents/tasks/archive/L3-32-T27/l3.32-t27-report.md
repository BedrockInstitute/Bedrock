## 4. The seam between the two probes

The seam is the number neither probe could give, and it is **negative: about 57
lines saved** against the 645-line sum of the in-scope halves. The savings, in
order of size:

1. **One import block instead of two (~17 lines).** The two probes each carried a
   near-identical import block (~35 lines combined, heavily overlapping on
   `FOL.Syntax`, `Cubical.Data.Nat(.Order)`, `FinData`, `Empty`); the chapter has
   one block of 17 lines. This is pure seam.
2. **Reuse of the delivered occurrence machinery (~35 lines).** The count probe
   re-derived `nconstT`/`nconst`/`constsT`/`consts`/`_++V_` (about 35 non-blank
   lines) even though `FOL.Manipulation.Parameters` delivers exactly those as
   `countTm`/`countFo`/`constantsTm`/`constantsFo` with the library's `Vec` append.
   The chapter imports them (C-14: host once). The one new local lemma
   `vecToList-++` stays, restated with the library append renamed `_++ᵥ_`.
3. **Section framing absorbed into prose (the remainder).** The probes' block-comment
   section banners and headers become prose, and the chapter's fence comments are
   limited to the two genuinely load-bearing ones (the `s m n e e'` legend and the
   marker index): 4 comment lines in the whole chapter against about 34 in the two
   probe sections.

The margin to the 700 stop-line is 112 lines, so the seam's negative cost is real
headroom, not scope creep; nothing from the in-scope halves was cut, and the two
half-sized deliverables (the count's decode and reconstruction, the shape-count's
injectivity) are complete.

## 5. Walls by LESSONS class

No wall events: every check of the chapter ran sub-second (cold 1.01 s wall / 0.86 s
user; warm ~0.9 s), no heap pressure, no normalization blowup. The two walls the
probes recorded are carried forward as standing obligations for the chapter's
consumers, and the chapter preserves both probes' cures:

- **I-4 / I-5-family (conversion wall at a concrete carrier):** T9's wall was
  instantiating the count at `K = ⟪ A ⟫`, which ran past 240 s. The chapter keeps
  T9's cure: everything lives inside `module Count (K : Type ℓ)` with `K` abstract,
  and `count-inj` is stated generically (strictly stronger than the target). The
  cardinal chapter must instantiate through this interface and must not inline the
  machinery at the presentation type.
- **I-4-family (defined pairing's implicits):** T17's unification failure on the
  defined `pair`'s implicit arguments. The chapter inherits the cure: `pair-inj`
  is invoked with all arguments explicit at every call site.

Three new measured facts, none a wall, surfaced for the owner as candidate craft
lessons (the law book says to surface rather than enter without an owner ruling;
glossary and law edits are outside this dispatch's write scope):

1. **A nested module cannot shadow an outer definition in the same file.**
   Agda 2.8 reports `ClashingDefinition` for `module Count`'s `code` against the
   outer shape-count `code` (reproduced in isolation). The two probes never met
   this because each file had only one module with that name. Cure used: rename
   one side (`code` → `encode` for the count's packaged injection, keeping the
   shape-count's `code` which matches `FOL.Coding`'s usage). Same event for the
   shape-count's `private go` against the count's `where go` blocks (`go` →
   `peel`).
2. **`using (x) renaming (x to y)` is rejected** as `RepeatedNamesInImportDirective`;
   the house pattern `using () renaming ( _++_ to _++ᵥ_ )` (already in
   `Base.Prelude`'s `Powerset` import) works.
3. **A transcription defect, caught cheaply:** the first pass dropped `[]` from the
   List import (the probe has `List; []; _∷_; _++_`), which broke the
   `vecToList-snoc` round trip with a confusing `Vec … zero !=< List K` error; the
   typechecker caught it in under two seconds. Recorded as evidence that the
   chapter's content is the probes' content, and that the per-line transcription
   was otherwise faithful.
