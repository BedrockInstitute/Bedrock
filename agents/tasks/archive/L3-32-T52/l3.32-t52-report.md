# [L3.32-T52] The carried sequence: one object, two residues, and whether it has a cheaper route

**Verdict, compressed. T45's itemization survives in shape but not in three of
its load-bearing details, and the corrections make the route CHEAPER, not
deader.** (1) The two residues do bottom out in one missing object-shape: a
collection of one tower's values as a member of the other tower's level. Each
residue is then a short corollary of its instance of that shape: Q-lim is
`Lset l = ⋃ F ∈ Sset (γ l)` from the L-stage family `F = {Lset δ : δ < l}` in
the level, and below-lim is `Sset β = ⋃ W ∈ Lset γ` from the S-level family
`W = {Sset (sucV δ) : δ < β}` in the stage. The two instances are the same
shape but two tower-story builds: the L-tower's story over the rud carrier
(Q-lim) and the S-tower's story over an L-stage (below-lim), so "one purchase,
not two" is right about the shared machinery and wrong about the per-tower
content. (2) The delivered face already carries the statement layer: at any
carrier with the telescope filled, `defSet σ` IS the set of values of honest
segments in the binding set, and `full-switch-⊇` lands it one level up. What
it does not carry is the binding-set collection in the carrier, the per-tower
story, the strengthened domain bound, and (a caveat) Devlin's successor-value
clause. (3) The cheapest formulation is the Devlin-shape one: quantify the
honest segments over the carrier itself with the strengthened bound
`dom f ∈ l`, which removes the need for the segment collection to be a carrier
member. That formulation needs `l ∈ Sset (U l)`, and T45's claim that this is
false is itself false: classically `J_α ∩ On = ω·α` (dev6.txt:1521) with
`S_{ω·α} = J_α` (dev6.txt:1565), so for every limit `l`, `l ∈ Sset (U l)`;
T45's example `l = ω² ∉ Sset (ω³)` is wrong (`ω² ∈ J_{ω²} ∩ On = ω³`). The
tree lacks the proof (the sandwich is recorded, not proved,
`src/L/Rud/Bridge.lagda.md:454-456`), so the bound-nameability is a new,
moderate lemma rather than an impossibility. T45's count-2 counter-instance is
also defective as written (its range values lie in `Lset l`), though the
underlying claim survives with a corrected witness. (4) The gate: at `l = ω`,
prove the crux family `F_ω = {Lset n : n < ω} ∈ Sset (γ ω)` and the bound arm
`ω ∈ Sset (U ω)`, one dispatch, 350-line stop-line. (5) Re-priced: Q-lim
about 610-990 naive / 1.2-2.2k calibrated, below-lim about 190-400 naive /
0.5-1.0k calibrated, combined about 0.8-1.4k naive / 1.7-3.2k calibrated,
with the crux's general-l transfer the dominant ×3 term the gate narrows.
Overage against the 25k reference, stated as a plain number and nothing else:
against the recorded endpoint band 19.85-26.87k naive, this purchase moves the
pessimistic corner to about 28.3k, roughly +3.3k over the line.

## 1. Is it really one object?

**T45's claim is directionally right, under-specified, and over-claimed. The
two residues bottom out in one missing object-SHAPE, not one missing object.**

The two targets, at the intended generality
(`src/L/Rud/Bridge.lagda.md:1092-1095` and `:1101-1105`):

- `Q-lim`: at a limit `l`, the tower hypothesis forces `Lset l ∈ Sset (γ l)`.
- `below-lim`: for a limit `β ∈ γ` (γ limit), `Sset β ∈ Lset γ`.

Both are "one tower's level is a member of the other tower's level above it"
facts at limits. Classically they are Devlin's 2.3 limit step (`L_λ ∈ J_{λ+1}`,
`_build/literature/dev6.txt:1655-1677`) and the `P(α)` content of VI.2.5
(`S_v ∈ L_{ωα}` for `v < ωα`, `dev6.txt:1725-1760`). T45's phrasing, "the
carried sequence of one tower as an element of the other tower's level", is
the right family for both.

**What the "one object" claim is missing: each residue is a short corollary
of a collection-in-level fact for ITS tower, and the two collections are two
instances of the same shape, not one theorem.**

Q-lim. `Lset l = ⋃_{δ<l} Lset δ` at a limit, and each member of the family
`F = {Lset δ : δ < l}` lies in `Sset (γ l)` by the tower hypothesis and the
index monotonicity (`γδ⊆U` :894, `Sset-⊆-mono` :907). If `F ∈ Sset (γ l)`,
then `Lset l = ⋃ F ∈ Sset (γ l)` by the union operation `F5` at the rud-closed
J-level `Sset (γ l)` (`Jset-rud`, `src/L/Rud/Step.lagda.md:954`; `γ l` is a
limit by `γ-lim`, `Bridge:742`). So:

```
Q-lim  ⟸  F = { Lset δ : δ < l } ∈ Sset (γ l)   [plus ~40-60 lines of
        ⋃-assembly: the delivered Lset-union-limit/in (Bridge:994-1010),
        F5 at the J-level, the equality ⋃ F = Lset l]
```

below-lim. `Sset β = ⋃_{δ<β} Sset (sucV δ)` at a limit (`Sset-union-*`,
`Bridge:1012-1028`), and each member of `W = {Sset (sucV δ) : δ < β}` lies in
`Lset γ` by the delivered successor memberships of the `Below` induction
(`Bridge:1234-1268`). If `W ∈ Lset γ`, then `Sset β = ⋃ W ∈ Lset γ` by the
union-of-a-member-of-an-L-stage argument (a definable union over a definable
set is definable, so the union of a member of a stage is in the stage). So:

```
below-lim  ⟸  W = { Sset (sucV δ) : δ < β } ∈ Lset γ
          [plus ~40-100 lines of union assembly]
```

So both residues reduce to "the collection of one tower's values in the other
tower's level", and each is a short corollary of its instance. T45's "one
purchase, not two" is right in the strong sense that the two prices share the
same dominant missing shape, the same delivered statement layer (the face)
and the same landing lemmas. It is wrong in the weak sense that the two
instances are two tower-story builds:

- Q-lim's instance needs the **L-tower's story at the rud carrier**: the
  honest-segment characterization of "`y = Lset δ`" over `J_l = Sset (U l)`,
  i.e. the level-formula five-clause story ported to the rud carrier (T36's
  measured 316-380 in-fence lines, carried over per the carrier-independence
  claims of T36 §4 and `LevelSigma:20-28`), plus the strengthened bound.
- below-lim's instance needs the **S-tower's story at an L-stage**: the
  honest-segment characterization of "`y = Sset δ`" over `Lset γ`, whose
  step is the rud step rather than the definable power. That story is not
  built anywhere; its ingredients exist as delivered facts
  (`values∈L`/`stepSet∈L`, `src/L/Rud/StepInL.lagda.md:370-371`, `:2419-2422`),
  but the object-language tower story over an L carrier is a new build.

The two builds share the face's statement layer and the assembly pattern; the
tower story itself is per-tower. So the honest purchase is "the
sequence-in-level machinery, instantiated twice", not "one object, proved
once".

**Which is cheaper?** Per-instance, below-lim's is the lighter build: its
target is loose (any limit `γ > β` suffices, so the collection may land at any
convenient L-stage above `β`), it rides the delivered `StepInL` machinery, and
its tower story (the rud step over L) is expressible from the delivered
sixteen descriptions. Q-lim's instance is the heavier build: the target is an
exact landing (`Sset (γ l)`), the tower story is the LevelFormula port at the
rud carrier, and the strengthened bound needs `l ∈ Sset (U l)` (a new lemma).
But both are dominated by the same missing shape, so the purchase order moves
less than the per-instance difference suggests: the sequence-in-level shape
should be built generically (P-h), then instantiated at both towers.

## 2. Does the delivered face already carry part of it?

**Yes for the statement layer, no for the binding set, the tower story, the
bound, and (a caveat) the successor-value clause.**

What the face gives, exactly. At any carrier `u` with binding set `K` and the
four telescope entries filled, the two-way adequacy is
(`src/L/InitialSegment.lagda.md:227-229`):

```
face-iff : (m : ⟪ u ⟫) → ⟨ ⟪ u ⟫↪ m ∈ˢ defSet σ ⟩ ⟷ Elem m
Elem m   = ∥ Σ[ f ∈ SM ] ( ⟨ fst f ∈ˢ fst (ι K) ⟩ × Approx u (fst f)
          × DefStep u (fst f) × ((⟪ u ⟫↪ m) ∈ran fst f) ) ∥₁
```

So `defSet σ` is, by construction, the set of values of honest initial
segments lying in the binding set `K` (the second-component range read,
`_∈ran_`, `InitialSegment:84`). At the carrier `Sset (U l)` with `K` the
segments, that IS the family of stages `F = {Lset δ : δ < l}` as a definable
set over the carrier. The landing lemma is delivered:
`LimitFullSwitch.full-switch-⊇` (`src/L/Rud/SatSets.lagda.md:1650-1651`)
puts `defSet (Sset β) φ ∈ Sset α` for limit `β ∈ α`, so the family lands in
`Sset (γ l)` the moment it is carved at `Sset (U l)`. The range operation at a
J-level is also delivered (`ranOp`, `J-ran`, `SatSets:151-152`, `:344-345`),
which is what the `⋃ F` assembly consumes.

So the face already carries the *statement layer* of the carried-sequence
object: "the family of stages is the definable set at the carrier, and it
lands one level up". What it does NOT carry:

1. **The binding-set collection in the carrier.** `K` is a module parameter
   `K : ⟪ u ⟫` (`InitialSegment:120`), a member of the carrier. For the
   composition at `Sset (U l)`, `K` must be the collection of honest segments
   `{seg_ν : ν < l}`, and `K ∈ Sset (U l)` is not delivered and not implied:
   given the tower's definability over the carrier (the machinery in
   question), `K` is Σ₁-definable over `J_l = Sset (U l)` (each `seg_ν ∈ J_l`
   by the induction hypothesis), hence `K ∈ Def(J_l) ⊆ J_{l+1} = Sset (γ l)`,
   one level up. Whether `K ∈ J_l` is exactly the sharp collection question,
   and it is the crux, not a corollary. The individual segments `seg_ν ∈ J_l` for
   `ν < l` are available from the carried-sequence theorem at the smaller
   index plus monotonicity, which is why T45's item 3 understates the object:
   the segments individually in the carrier are not the binding set in the
   carrier.
2. **The tower story at the new carrier.** The face's `Approx` is a telescope
   slot (`InitialSegment:184-230`). At the rud carrier the delivered entry is
   the three-clause fragment (`LevelSigma:128-130`); the limit clause and the
   ordinal domain bound are the omitted pieces (`LevelSigma:20-28`). Filling
   them is the LevelSigma port of the LevelFormula story, priced by T36 at
   the real tower (148 + 168) and transferred per the carrier-independence
   claims (`T36 §4`).
3. **The strengthened domain bound.** The delivered `domOrd` says the domain
   of `f` is an ordinal in the carrier (`LevelFormula:139-144`). Devlin's
   formula bounds the domain by the limit index itself: `dom(f) ∈ α`
   (`dev6.txt:1659-1662`). The composition's out direction needs the bound
   `δ < l`; the weak delivered clause allows `δ ∈ [l, U l)` and the escape is
   real (see section 3). The face's bounded-`K` design cannot express the
   strengthened bound without either the collection `K` in the carrier or a
   formula variant.
4. **The successor-value clause (caveat).** The face's Def-step entry is
   `DefStep u f = "f is a definable set at some member of u"`
   (`InitialSegment:90-95`), which collapses to `⊤̇` at a level
   (`LevelFormula:267-268`). Devlin's successor clause is the value relation
   `f(v) = Def(f(v-1))` (`dev6.txt:1662-1663`). These are different clauses:
   the delivered story never pins successor VALUES of an honest segment. For
   the K-collection route this does not matter (K itself pins the values);
   for the Devlin-shape route (section 3) it does, and the value relation must
   be written and decoded at the rud carrier. This is a design decision the
   delivered chapters do not settle, priced at 0-100 lines below.

**What a third instantiation at this carrier costs.** The face itself costs
nothing new (it is delivered and carrier-generic, P-h; `InitialSegment:14-21`).
The pair kit is consumed, not re-derived (T20's 193 shared lines already
charged once). The collapse and the landing are delivered (`LevelSigma:409-410`,
`SatSets:1650-1651`). So a third instantiation at `Sset (U l)` is the
LevelSigma five-clause port: the two missing clauses with their two-way
decodes at T36's measured prices (316-380, transferred per T36 §4), plus the
strengthened bound (a modification of `domOrd`/`domForm`, ~20-60) and, for
the Devlin-shape variant, the successor-value clause (0-100). Compared with
T45's from-scratch itemization, the face's genericity saves the whole
statement layer (T20's measurement: 111 per-consumer lines once the shared kit
exists, and the face itself is already wired), so the "third instantiation"
is meaningfully cheaper than a from-scratch composition.

## 3. Is there a cheaper formulation of the same mathematical content?

**Yes: the Devlin-shape formulation, which replaces the K-collection crux
with a bound-nameability lemma that T45 wrongly believed impossible.**

### 3a. The bound is nameable. T45's example is wrong.

T45 claims: "the carrier cannot name the bound `l` (`l ∉ Sset (U l)` in
general, e.g. `l = ω²` with `U l = ω³`), so the domain bound cannot be
written into the formula at all" (T45 §1). Both the general claim and the
example are false, classically:

- `J_α ∩ On = ω·α` (`rank(J_α) = J_α ∩ On = ωα`,
  `_build/literature/dev6.txt:1521`): the ordinals in the J-level at index
  `α` are exactly those below `ω·α`.
- `J_α = S_{ω·α}` (`J_{α+1} = rud(J_α) = ⋃ S^n(J_α) = ⋃ S_{ωα+n} =
  S_{ωα+ω}`, `dev6.txt:1565`): the project's `Sset` (one rud round per
  index) satisfies `Sset (ω·α) = J_α`.
- The closed form `U l = ⋃_{δ<l} γ δ = ω·l` for limit `l` is the tower's own
  arithmetic (`γ(β) = ω·(β+1)`, `Bridge:648-660`).

So `Sset (U l) = Sset (ω·l) = J_l`, and `l ∈ J_l ∩ On = ω·l` because `l < ω·l`
for limit `l`. For the named example: `Sset (ω³) = J_{ω²}` and
`J_{ω²} ∩ On = ω·ω² = ω³ ∋ ω²`. **`ω² ∈ Sset (ω³)`; T45's example is
inverted.** The bound `l` is nameable at the natural index for every limit
`l`.

What the tree lacks is the proof: no S-tower analog of `ord∈Lset-suc`
(`src/L/Ordinal/Stages.lagda.md:434`) exists, and the sandwich is recorded as
a classical fact, not proved (`Bridge:442-456`). So "nameable" means "true and
provable with a new, moderate lemma" (the S-tower's ordinal-content lemma,
whose first instance the gate measures), not "written into the formula is
impossible".

### 3b. The Devlin-shape formulation avoids the K-collection.

Devlin's own limit step does not collect segments into a binding set: it
quantifies the honest segments over the carrier itself,
`L_α = {x ∈ J_α : ∃f ∈ J_α [f honest ∧ dom f ∈ α ∧ x ∈ ⋃ ran f]}`
(`dev6.txt:1659-1662`, `:1665-1670`). The project's face bounds the witness by
`K` (`InitialSegment:131-132`); a formula variant with the unbounded
existential over the carrier plus the strengthened bound `dom f ∈ l` (a
constant naming `l`, legitimate once `l ∈ Sset (U l)`) needs:

1. `l ∈ Sset (U l)` (the bound-nameability lemma, section 3a);
2. the honest segments of length `< l` as carrier members (the carried
   sequence at the smaller index plus monotonicity: the induction hypothesis,
   not a new object);
3. the full tower story at the rud carrier, now including the successor-value
   clause (section 2 item 4), with two-way adequacy;
4. the range read (the delivered second-component read carves the family of
   stages; Devlin's own formula reads the union of the range, and either read
   plus the union assembly closes the residue);
5. the landing (`full-switch-⊇`) and the `⋃ F` assembly.

This is cheaper than the K-collection route in exactly one place: it replaces
the sharp, unsettled "`K = {seg_ν : ν < l} ∈ Sset (U l)`" with the
provable "`l ∈ Sset (U l)`". The price of the replacement is the ordinal
content lemma (section 3a), which is a known, moderate shape rather than an
open sharp question.

### 3c. The other candidates from the brief.

- **Bound as a parameter**: the face's `σ` is arity 1 with no parameter slots
  (`InitialSegment:131`); parameters cannot be threaded through `defSet`. But
  the strengthened bound is expressible by naming `l` as a constant of the
  model, which is the "bound as a carrier element" reading of 3b, and it works
  once `l ∈ Sset (U l)`.
- **Member of a larger carrier**: for Q-lim, the composition at `Sset (γ l)`
  makes the landing overshoot (`defSet (Sset (γ l)) σ ∈ Sset (γ (γ l))`, far
  above the target `Sset (γ l)`), so this candidate fails for the exact
  landing Q-lim needs. For below-lim the target is loose (any limit `γ > β`),
  so a larger carrier is free; this is one reason below-lim's instance is
  cheaper.
- **Truncated existential**: the crux objects are sets (families, segments),
  not propositions; the truncated-existential pattern (T43) does not build
  them. It is usable only in the assembly's propositional goals, which are
  already cheap.
- **Different index**: unnecessary once 3a is known: the natural index already
  names the bound, so no residue restatement at a higher index is required.
- **The direct-corollary assembly (new)**: both residues are ~40-100 lines
  past their collection-in-level instance (`⋃ F`/`⋃ W` plus closure inside the
  target level, section 1). For the family route, T45's union-of-range read
  (35-60) drops from Q-lim's route: the family is carved with the delivered
  second-component read, and the stage is the union of the family. T45's item
  2 is an artifact of the face-composition formulation, not of the residue.

### 3d. T45's count-2 counter-instance is defective as written.

T45's counter-instance to the three-clause out direction: "with `K = u`, the
two-pair set `{pr ∅ ∅, pr (Lset δ₀) (Lset δ₀)}` satisfies all three clauses
and `DefStep` for any carrier member `Lset δ₀`, yet its range is a single
stage, and nothing forces a general `x ∈ ran f` to be a stage below `l`"
(T45 §1, count 2). Two readings, both failing:
