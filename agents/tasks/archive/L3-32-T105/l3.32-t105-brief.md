## ATTACK THE DIAGNOSIS FIRST, BECAUSE EVERYTHING ELSE RESTS ON IT

The proposal claims the failure was **not** rule overload but a **stale
citation habit**: the orchestrator picks rule IDs from memory, and when
`Rule 1` to `Rule 20` and `P-i` entered the corpus on 2026-08-01 and 08-02,
the habit never updated, so the playbook was cited in **10 of 112 briefs, nine
of them today**.

**Verify that count yourself.** The claim is checkable:

```sh
ls _build/briefs/*.md | wc -l
grep -lE '\bRule [0-9]+|P-i\b' _build/briefs/*.md
grep -ohE '\b(P-[a-l]|R-[0-9]+|Rule [0-9]+|I-[0-9]+|D-[0-9]+|C-[0-9]+)\b' _build/briefs/*.md | sort | uniq -c | sort -rn
git log --diff-filter=A --format="%ad %h" --date=short -S "### Rule 1." -- dev/LESSONS.md
```

Then attack the INFERENCE, which is the weaker part:

- **Is a low citation count actually evidence of non-application?** A rule can
  be followed without being cited: it may be in the agent's training, or
  implied by another cited rule, or simply how the author already writes.
  `[L3.32-T100]` swept the tree and found the trunk OUTSIDE the three expensive
  modules **already follows the playbook, with one violation in 58 modules.**
  **That is evidence AGAINST the proposal's story and the proposal does not
  address it.** Push on this hard: if the playbook is followed where it was
  never cited, what exactly did the citation gap cause?
- **Would citing the playbook have prevented the actual defects?** Check.
  `SquareLaw`'s cost was concrete `fiber` applications in bodies; `Bridge`'s
  was a read lemma stated one layer early; `Condensation`'s was an assembly
  elaborating at a concrete formula. **Name the specific Rule that, if cited,
  would have stopped each one, and quote it.** If no rule in the playbook
  actually covers a defect, the proposal's premise fails for that defect and
  the real gap is a MISSING rule, not an unread one.
- **Is there a simpler sufficient explanation?** Nothing in this project could
  measure check cost until `[T86]` profiled the tree. Perhaps the modules were
  written without anyone knowing they were slow, and no amount of rule
  citation would have changed that, because the rules do not say "your module
  will take 856 seconds". Test this rival explanation seriously.

## THEN ATTACK THE MECHANISM

- **The 1,200-word cap on `dev/CORE.md`.** Is a cap enforceable in practice, or
  will it be raised the first time something important does not fit? What
  happens at the moment a rule must be evicted, and who decides? A cap with no
  eviction procedure is a wish.
- **Clause 2, the checker that refuses a LESSONS entry belonging to no
  bundle.** This is the proposal's load-bearing claim: it says the 2026-08-01
  commit would not have passed. **Verify that.** Would the sixteen Rules have
  been assignable to a bundle at the time, or would the author simply have
  assigned them all to `build` to pass the gate, producing a bundle so large
  it is the corpus again? **Bundle bloat is the obvious failure mode and the
  proposal does not defend against it.** Is there any cap on bundle size, and
  should there be?
- **`kind:` declared by the brief's own author.** The orchestrator both writes
  the brief and declares its kind, so it can pick the kind with the smallest
  bundle. Is that a real hole? What would close it?
- **`dev/rules.toml` as a second home for rule metadata.** AGENTS.md requires
  one canonical home per rule. Does splitting a rule's text (`LESSONS.md`) from
  its routing (`rules.toml`) violate that, and what drift does it invite when
  an entry is renamed, merged or withdrawn? **Note that a category was
  withdrawn from P-l TODAY**, which is exactly the drift case.
- **Cost.** The proposal estimates a day's dispatch and one `rules.py` call per
  brief. Is the recurring cost really that small, or does every brief now need
  a judgement about kind, a bundle review, and a checker argument?

## THE ALTERNATIVE YOU SHOULD CONSIDER AND COMPARE

does not mention: **a machine check that fails the thing directly.** The one

**Would N more checkers beat a rule-routing layer?** Argue it. If the honest

context engineering.
