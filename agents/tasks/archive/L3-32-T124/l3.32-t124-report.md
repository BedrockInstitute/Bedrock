## 6. Contradictions with the brief or T121

1. **The cure reproduces; direction agrees and magnitude beats the scratch.**
   My control (52,879 ms Images, 28,769 ms Step) matches `[T121]`'s warm
   controls (52,998 and 27,699); my cured totals (6,705 and 4,640) beat its
   scratch (7,951 and 5,110). My measurement wins where it differs, and it
   agrees in direction. Removal ~70.3 s total (Images 46.2 s + Step 24.1 s),
   against the brief's ~66 s claim.
2. **A top-level opaque seal must sit at column 0 or the names silently
   vanish from the interface.** My first transcription of the seal put the
   marker comment and `opaque` two spaces in (matching T123's nested Pairing
   seal); the module typechecked, delivered the full timing cure, and
   exited 0, but `left`, `left-spec` and `left-compute` were NOT exported:
   Step failed with `ModuleDoesntExport`/`NotInScope`, and a real importer
   probe reproduced it against the interface. Moving the comment and
   `opaque` to column 0 (the `[T121]` scratch shape) exported all three
   names and every consumer compiled. This is a finding for the owner: an
   opaque seal that measures perfectly can silently break every consumer
   through a missing export, and `make check` on the module alone would not
   have caught it; only the consumer re-check did.
3. **The seal exports three names, not exactly one read.** `left` and
   `left-spec` were already exported before the cure and stay exported
   byte-identical; the surface grows by exactly `left-compute` (the P-k read
   lemma), inside the same opaque block as `left`/`left-spec`, matching the
   measured scratch rather than the R-36 phrasing of "its own `opaque
   unfolding` block". Recorded for the owner, not a stop condition.
4. **StepInL consequences verified, not trusted.** The cure is ~6 lines
   cheaper than `[T121]` priced it (no bridge exists to write), and the
   StepInL spillover is not credited anywhere in this report (section 4).
   Nothing contradicts `[T121]`'s diagnosis: the hot rows before and after
