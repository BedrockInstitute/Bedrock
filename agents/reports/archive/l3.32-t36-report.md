## 3. The five-clause story is complete

`Approx u f` is now `pairhood f × singleValued f × zeroClause f × domOrd f ×
limitClause f`, `Ap` is the matching five-conjunct formula, and `ap-out` /
`ap-in` / `a-ok` walk all five. The face's `Approx` entry at this carrier is
no longer the pairhood fragment: it is the whole tower story, with each
clause written twice (meta level over the graph, object-language formula) and
decoded both ways. The exports added by this task: the meta predicates
`domOrd`, `isLimit`, `limitClause`; the formulas `isOrdAt`, `isLimitAt`,
`domForm`, `limPr`, `limIn`, `limOut`, `limitForm`; and the two-way decodes
`isOrd-out`/`in`, `isLimit-out`/`in`, `dom-out`/`in`, `limit-out`/`in`.

Two design points, both deliberate and both now checked:

- **The limit clause is constructively honest.** `isLimit a` is `IsOrd a`
  plus nonempty plus no-greatest-member, the reading that needs no dichotomy,
  and the union condition is stated at the graph level: whenever `pr a y`
  lies in `f` with `a` such a limit ordinal, `y` is exactly the union of the
  values below `a`, as the two inclusions (every member of `y` comes from
  some pair `pr b z` with `b` in `a`, and every value reached below `a` lands
  back in `y`). The chapter stays LEM-free end to end.
- **The domain bound is the exactness decode.** `domOrd f` names a witness
  `δ` in the carrier with `IsOrd δ` and both projections of the exactness
  (`a ∈ δ` iff some `pr a b ∈ f`), which is what makes the object clause and
  the meta read interchangeable rather than merely one-way.

The Def-step, the range read, the face instantiation and `read-off` are
unchanged in content; `read-off` now assembles the full five-clause story.

## 4. What the rud consumer can reuse, precisely

T32 reported the omitted clauses are carrier-independent; this build confirms
it by construction, because the two new pieces were written without a single
tower-specific fact. Concretely, the rud `LevelSigma` chapter reuses:

1. **The meta predicates verbatim.** `domOrd`, `isLimit`, `limitClause` are
   carrier-independent as stated; the only carrier touch in the whole block
   is `domOrd`'s witness conjunct `⟨ δ ∈ˢ u ⟩`, which at the rud carrier
   reads `δ ∈ˢ Jset α`, the same shape with `Sset-trans α` playing `utr`.
   The rud chapter can adopt the constructive `isLimit` as its premise
   (LEM-free) or keep its own classical `OrdArith.isLimit`; the clause
   itself does not care.
2. **The formula layer by carrier substitution.** `isOrdAt`, `isLimitAt`,
   `domForm`, `limPr`, `limIn`, `limOut`, `limitForm` are written entirely
   over the shared `PairKit` (`PK.prAt`) with the fixed arity conventions
   (arity 2 for the clause, arity 4 for the limit body environment
   `(y, a, f, x)`). The rud chapter already instantiates `PairKit` at its
   own carrier, so each formula ports by swapping the carrier, with zero
   re-derivation and the same de Bruijn indices.
3. **The decodes verbatim.** `isOrd-out`/`in`, `isLimit-out`/`in`,
   `dom-out`/`in`, `limit-out`/`in` use only: the carrier's transitivity
   (`utr`), the shared `pair∈` membership decode, `PM.pair-left`/`right`,
   and truncation walks. Nothing in them mentions `Lset`, rank, or LEM, so
   the same proof text typechecks at any transitive carrier carrying the
   kit. The rud chapter's `pair∈` is already byte-identical to this file's.
4. **The sharing inside the chapter.** The isOrd decode is shared between the
   domain bound and the limit premise here, and that sharing carries to the
   rud copy unchanged.

The one shared-shaped piece remains `pair∈`, the file's own 25-line decode
that T30 already flagged as a candidate to promote into `PairKit`; the two
new clauses consume it but do not multiply it.

**New measured datum, surfaced for the owner (not filed: `dev/LESSONS.md` is
outside this task's write scope).** In Agda 2.8.0, `where`-block declarations
are scope-checked sequentially: a declaration's type and body can only
reference **earlier** declarations in the same `where` block, and a forward
reference reports `[NotInScope]` at scope checking (reproduced on a minimal
standalone file: a `where`-local `g` calling a later `h` fails the same way).
The fix is to order where-helpers dependency-first. This cost two error
cycles (about three checks) here, in `isOrd-out`'s certificate helpers; it is
a candidate C-23 if the owner wants it recorded.

One design correction, not a wall: `limIn`/`limOut`/`limPr` were first written
in a `where` block of `limitForm`, but the decodes must name them, so they are
LevelAt-level definitions instead (part of the 27-line formula block above).
