# LJ-0.3: Retrospective on the route change of 2026-08-09

## 1. THE STRUGGLE, in one page

On 2026-08-09 the project executed the L-trophy configuration, ruled
2026-08-04 (`archive/dev/JOURNAL-archived.md:947`). The Def tower kept the
trophy. The GCH wing rode a fresh-generic Sigma-1 face. The rud code was
booked to retire.

Three facts dominated the record. First, the bridge kernel rested on a
classically false hypothesis. The per-level identification `Matching` and its
invariant `p2` (every L-stage a member of every limit S-level above it) fail
at the smallest instance, `L_{ω+1} ∈ J_2`. Devlin VI.2.4 gives the true
sandwich `L_α ⊆ J_α ⊆ L_{ω·α}` with equality only at the fixed points
(`archive/dev/JOURNAL-archived.md:580-600`). The corrective stop R4 recorded
the falsity with the counter-instance and kept the true half,
`bridge-isJ→isL`, unconditional from `p4`
(`archive/dev/JOURNAL-archived.md:1094-1100`). The route residue moved to
`BlockPowLim`, which is Devlin VI.2.3's successor step
(`archive/dev/JOURNAL-archived.md:606`).

Second, the check-cost campaign (D30, ruled 2026-08-06) measured the tree.
The surviving trunk checked slower than the retiring subtree by 4.2x per
obligation at the corrected caliber (`archive/dev/STATUS-archived.md:102`). The
abstract-carrier discipline failed to transfer twice and was refuted as a
general lever (`dev/LESSONS.md:2116-2143`). SquareLaw fell from 856 s to
64.4 s, a 13x cut for net zero lines
(`archive/dev/STATUS-archived.md:116`). The wall gate met at 487.9 s against
498 s on 2026-08-07 (`archive/dev/JOURNAL-archived.md:3593-3599`). The
campaign minted the content-class laws P-m and P-n: parameterized content
checks near 0.01 s per line and instantiation content near 0.22, a
twentyfold spread (`dev/LESSONS.md:2254-2276`).

Third, the AC endgame re-priced upward on every survey-class term. The
carried sequence measured 904 lines and 186 s against a 200-line floor
(`archive/dev/JOURNAL-archived.md:3469-3473`). The content rate measured 0.297 s
per line (`archive/dev/JOURNAL-archived.md:3493`). After the owner raised
the cap from 16,000 to 20,000 lines and the wall from 498 s to 600 s, the AC
endpoint still measured 20,463-22,143 naive against the cap, and the seconds
landing 704.7-717.5 s against the wall
(`archive/dev/DECISIONS-archived.md:59`). The deletion test passed
(`archive/dev/DECISIONS-archived.md:57`).

The same day, task T257 priced the two routes' double-trophy endpoints on
one caliber for the first time: internalization 25,485-28,258 naive against
rud 34,940-39,823, a gap of 9,455-11,565 with a declared weak point worth
4,238-5,218 (`archive/dev/JOURNAL-archived.md:3557-3560`). Task T260 then
closed the cheapest way to narrow the gap: the GCH wing does not consume the
bridge rows on import-graph strength, so the bridge does not pay for itself
from the wing's side (`archive/dev/JOURNAL-archived.md:3569-3573`). Task
T259 named a viable crossing at 0.9-1.8k against the 5,047-line rebuild, but
its probe never ran (`archive/dev/TASKS-archived.md:264-266`).

So the project was stuck in this position on 2026-08-09. The AC trophy was
one lever from its line cap, and its seconds miss was measured. The GCH
wing's survey-class terms had re-priced upward repeatedly. The first honest
comparison of the two routes showed the route being built 9-11.5k over the
alternative, with the bridge serving neither trophy's wing. The route change
was the owner's answer to that position.

INFER: the change tried to solve the repeated up-pricing and the growing
totals by switching to the textbook architecture, the two towers and the
bridge, with maximum reuse as the lever that reduces the total. The record
states the ruling's core constraint in those words
(`dev/memos/L3.32-route-adjustment-conflicts.md:2-7`).

## 2. VERDICT ON THE DECISION

**Was changing the route right?** The direction was defensible; the timing
was not. The record supports the architecture. The J tower and the
two-directional bridge were delivered, the fine-structure literature is
J-native (`archive/dev/DECISIONS-archived.md:40`), and the check-cost laws
favor generic content. The record does not
support ruling the change before its own benchmark and reuse measurement
existed. D26 had ruled that a number alone may never put the campaign route
back on the table (`archive/dev/DECISIONS-archived.md:47`). D39 reversed
that on the strength of T257, a single pass the same row declared not clear
enough to bind (`dev/ledger.toml:135-180`). I judge the change premature,
not wrong in direction. INFER: the owner treated the architecture as the
primary reason and the arithmetic as the consequence to be made to work.

**Was this route the right one?** As an architecture, yes. The two towers
and the bridge are what the book claims, the two-definition identification
is its content, and D19 already ruled the bridge two-directional
(`archive/dev/DECISIONS-archived.md:41`). As a priced route, no. The only
comparison available showed it 9,455-11,565 naive over the benchmark, and
the cheaper fix, the wing riding the bridge, was refuted by import graph
(`archive/dev/JOURNAL-archived.md:3569-3573`). The route is a bet on
unmeasured reuse. The record does not show that bet to be false. It shows
that the bet was made before it was priced.

**Were the constraints right?** The relative form is better than the
absolute form it replaced. The absolute numbers had drifted origins: the
16,000 cap was the optimistic end of an unprobed survey
(`archive/dev/TASKS-archived.md:263`), and the 7.5-minute gate was an
unsupported figure corrected to 8.3 (`archive/dev/DECISIONS-archived.md:50`).
The relative constraints with machine-enforced suspension flags are honest
(`dev/ledger.toml:83-135`). Two defects remain. The line benchmark has one
pass with a declared weak point and no re-measure
(`dev/ledger.toml:135-148`). DD4, the route's core constraint, has no
measurement at all (`_build/lj-0.2-sufficiency.md:36-52`). A rule with no
enforcement point is a wish by the project's own standard
(`dev/PLAN.md:162`). The time benchmark does not exist, because the
internalization route never built GCH (`dev/PLAN.md:154`).

## 3. WELL / BADLY / INSUFFICIENTLY / EXCESSIVELY

### What was done WELL

1. **The archive was built before the change, and the change used it.** Task
   T13 built the archive infrastructure with a poison-pill verification
   (`archive/dev/JOURNAL-archived.md:1312-1322`). The route change archived
   the 72 differing files and the whole diff, unedited
   (`archive/rud-route/README.md:1-45`). DD18 then made surveying the archive
   a brief section and a return section (`dev/PLAN.md:163`). The retired
   route's measurements survive as reference, not as loss.

2. **The false kernel was corrected honestly.** The R4 corrective stop
   recorded `Matching` as classically false with the Devlin citation and the
   counter-instance, and kept every true asset
   (`archive/dev/JOURNAL-archived.md:1094-1100`). D-10's appended text made
   the truth check of a named classical lemma mandatory
   (`dev/LESSONS.md:1333-1360`). The correction is the campaign's best work,
   and the new route inherits it: the bridge's delivered half is
   unconditional.

3. **The check-cost campaign settled its caliber before its thresholds.** F0
   corrected its own wrong first answer the same day
   (`archive/dev/JOURNAL-archived.md:2187-2195`). The premise was tested
   three times, and the two failures were recorded as refutations, not
   hidden. The exit condition carried no projected number, because a
   threshold that flips with the measurement is not a gate
   (`archive/dev/DECISIONS-archived.md:50`). The freeze was proportionate in
   duration and output: two days, a 13x cut on the worst module, six laws,
   and measured refusals on the rest.

4. **The relative constraints are honest about what is not known.** The
   suspension flags are read by the checkers that apply them
   (`_build/lj-0.1-consistency.md:268-288`). The 25k reference was admitted
   to be a coincidence with T257's band, not evidence
   (`dev/ledger.toml:120-135`). The re-arm condition is named in the ledger
   (`dev/ledger.toml:112-118`). That is the right shape for a benchmark that
   does not exist yet.

5. **DD24's ratio bar is the right instrument for the wing.** A ratio cannot
   be met by writing less of a worse thing (`dev/PLAN.md:165`). It is
   enforced staged by `check-ratio.py`, and P-m and P-n explain why the rate
   is a content-class certificate (`dev/LESSONS.md:2254-2301`).

6. **The consolidation kept every old citation true.** The D series and the
   L3.32-T series were archived whole, not renumbered, and
   `check-rule-ids.py` resolves old codes against the archives
   (`archive/dev/DECISIONS-archived.md:1-15`). Hundreds of citations in the
   journal and the commit history still mean what they meant.

### What was done BADLY

1. **The change inverted D26's anti-referendum rule on a benchmark declared
   unfit to bind.** D26 ruled that a number alone may never put the route
   back on the table (`archive/dev/DECISIONS-archived.md:47`). D39 reversed
   that the same day it declared T257's pass not clear enough to bind the
   new constraints (`dev/ledger.toml:135-148`). The same figure cannot carry
   both judgments. INFER: if the benchmark was not clear enough to bind the
   route's constraints, it was not clear enough to overturn the route
   either. The change should have waited for LJ-2.1's measurement.

2. **The change froze a near-complete trophy.** Task T262, the critical path
   of the AC chain, was frozen before it launched
   (`archive/dev/TASKS-archived.md:267`). The deletion test had passed, and
   the endpoint was within one lever of the cap
   (`archive/dev/DECISIONS-archived.md:59`). The change demoted that AC
   proof to a benchmark baseline and moved the trophy to phase 3
   (`dev/PLAN.md:397`). The record does not show the AC chain was blocking
   the wing. It shows the opposite: the wing waited on the archival
   (`archive/dev/DECISIONS-archived.md:50`).

3. **The document rebuild broke the gate it was supposed to rest on.** The
   AGENTS.md refresh added about 420 words and nobody ran the checker, so
   `make check` went red with the change (`_build/lj-0.1-consistency.md:24-34`).
   The standing brief clause still pinned the retired route R2' into every
   future brief (`_build/lj-0.1-consistency.md:90-104`). A phase-1 brief
   written to the rules would have sent an agent to the wrong route
   (`_build/lj-0.2-sufficiency.md:22-28`). The status screen described the
   retired route for the whole day of the change
   (`_build/lj-0.1-consistency.md:107-124`).

4. **The consolidation dropped a live rule.** DD17 lost the emergency
   breakthrough tier while ORCHESTRATION still pointed at DD17 for it
   (`_build/lj-0.1-consistency.md:105-116`). A rule with no home violates
   DD19's one-home rule in the very commit series that implemented DD19.

5. **The suspension machinery was half-applied.** `check-timing.py` prints
   the suspension and then enforces anyway (`_build/lj-0.1-consistency.md:41-55`).
   `deletion-test.py` reads one flag and ignores the other
   (`_build/lj-0.1-consistency.md:170-182`). `dashboard.py` reads no flag at
   all (`_build/lj-0.1-consistency.md:183-195`). Three flags, three
   half-readers. The project's own standard names this class: a claim of
   enforcement that no checker delivers.

### What was done INSUFFICIENTLY

1. **The reuse dividend was never measured before the ruling.** The reuse
   map is LJ-3.1, planned, in phase 3 (`dev/PLAN.md:392`). DD8 says a build
   brief that cannot name its widest unmeasured term is not ready to send
   (`dev/PLAN.md:155`). The route itself was the build, and its widest term,
   the reuse dividend, was not gated. The one comparison available showed
   the gap widening, not closing (`archive/dev/JOURNAL-archived.md:3557-3573`).

2. **The probe that would decide the economics never ran.** Task T261, the
   probe for the S-tower crossing, was queued for an Agda slot and never
   launched (`archive/dev/TASKS-archived.md:266`). T257's weak point, a
   fifth of the band, was declared and left open
   (`dev/ledger.toml:141-148`). The decision's economics rest on both.

3. **DD4 refused even the report, not just the gate.** A shared-line count
   as a pass-or-fail would be gamed, and the owner's refusal of that is
   right. But the machinery to report shared lines already exists in
   `ledger.py`'s import closures (`_build/lj-0.2-sufficiency.md:36-52`). A
   report with no gate cannot be gamed and can still be read. The record
   shows the refusal went one step too far: it refused the measurement that
   the route's core constraint depends on, and the sufficiency audit named
   the consequence (`_build/lj-0.2-sufficiency.md:36-52`).

4. **The benchmark has a circularity the record names but does not close.**
   Phase 1 builds the internalization GCH wing, and LJ-2.1 measures it to
   set the benchmark the two-tower route must beat (`dev/PLAN.md:381-390`).
   DD24 guards the wing's ratio but not its total
   (`dev/PLAN.md:165`). A long cheap wing sets a high benchmark. The record
   names the risk for seconds; nothing guards the line total the same way.

### What was done EXCESSIVELY

1. **The governance rewrite was over-scoped for one day.** Thirteen commits
   rebuilt PLAN, ORCHESTRATION, AGENTS.md, the ledger, three archives and
   the task codes, and the rest of the twenty repaired them
   (`git log --oneline faf02fc..HEAD`). The consolidation merged 25 rows
   into 15, revoked DD7, absorbed D29 into DD4, and dropped the emergency
   tier. The audits found 30 defects, six load-bearing
   (`_build/lj-0.1-consistency.md:9-22`). The amendment path D30 used
   (amendments A to G recorded, never rewritten) would have produced fewer
   defects and kept every rule. INFER: the route changed the architecture,
   not the rulebook's subject matter; the full rebuild was a choice, not a
   necessity.

2. **The task-code renumbering was a second carve-out without a ruling.**
   Rule 3 says a code is never renamed and the one carve-out is spent
   (`dev/PLAN.md:185-191`). The LJ1-T to LJ-<phase>.<step> renumbering on
   2026-08-09 violated that without amending the rule
   (`_build/lj-0.1-consistency.md:238-253`). It left 17 stale task labels
   and seven stale codes in live documents (`_build/lj-0.1-consistency.md:196-215`).

3. **The day's commits were all documents and tooling; none verified the
   change.** The route change's own consistency audit was dispatched as a
   task afterward (`git log faf02fc..HEAD`). The orchestrator's return
   audit (`dev/ORCHESTRATION.md:278-330`) was not applied to the
   orchestrator's own commits. The one piece of work that skipped the
   project's verification protocol was the piece that changed everything.

4. **The freeze's gate had zero margin by construction.** The 498 s figure
   was the campaign's own projection, and the owner recorded that the gate
   carried the projection's error inside it
   (`archive/dev/JOURNAL-archived.md:3593-3599`). Meeting it proved the
   campaign's arithmetic, not the tree's health. The owner corrected this
   twice, first to 8.3 minutes and then to the relative form. The
   correction was right; the original gate design was the over-reach.

## 4. VERDICT ON THE EXECUTION

The thirty defects and the sufficiency gaps say one thing: the change was
executed as a document rewrite, not as a state change with verification.

The evidence for that reading is concrete. The gate went red with the
rebuild itself (`_build/lj-0.1-consistency.md:24-34`). The document that
generates every brief stated the wrong route
(`_build/lj-0.1-consistency.md:90-104`). The status screen, whose only
purpose is to be read at the moment of change, described the retired route
for the whole day (`_build/lj-0.1-consistency.md:107-124`). The suspension
machinery was built with three half-readers
(`_build/lj-0.1-consistency.md:41-55,170-195`). The consolidation dropped a
live rule (`_build/lj-0.1-consistency.md:105-116`). Each defect is a
document or a checker. None is mathematics.

The sufficiency audit adds the structural half. The switch was sufficient
for phase 1 only after LJ-0.1's repairs landed
(`_build/lj-0.2-sufficiency.md:17-22`). The route's core constraint had no
measure, no declaration and no checker (`_build/lj-0.2-sufficiency.md:36-52`).
ORCHESTRATION section 5.1 still described the retired resume order
(`_build/lj-0.2-sufficiency.md:61-68`). The ledger's dispatch queue listed
the retired route (`_build/lj-0.2-sufficiency.md:69-75`). The wing's fate
after measurement was unstated (`_build/lj-0.2-sufficiency.md:83-92`).

The deepest point is the audit pattern itself. The project audits every
dispatched agent's return (`dev/ORCHESTRATION.md:278-330`). The route
change's thirteen commits were the one piece of work that skipped that
audit until LJ-0.1 ran afterward. A change that rewrites the rulebook needs
the rulebook's own gate more than any other change does. It did not get it.

INFER: the execution treated the route change as finished when the documents
said the new route. The tree, the flags, the checkers and the briefs had to
agree, and nothing verified the agreement. The repairs fixed the mechanics;
the sufficiency gaps show the structure was never completed.

## 5. WHAT I WOULD HAVE DECIDED

On 2026-08-09, with full decision authority and only the record, I would
not have ruled the two-tower bridge route that day.

My decision, concretely: keep the L-trophy configuration as the working
route. Finish the AC chain first. Dispatch T262, the Q-lim general
transfer, whose shape the record already gated green
(`archive/dev/JOURNAL-archived.md:3577-3579`). Then land `L ⊨ AC` on the
delivered tree. The record measured that trophy one lever from its cap,
with the deletion test passed (`archive/dev/DECISIONS-archived.md:57-59`).

Then build the internalization GCH wing exactly as phase 1 of the new route
plans it, over the delivered coding tower (`dev/PLAN.md:381-390`). Measure
the internalization double-trophy endpoint in lines and seconds
(`dev/PLAN.md:390-391`). Then rule the final architecture at LJ-2.1,
against the measured benchmark, with the two-tower bridge priced as the
candidate and the reuse map completed first (`dev/PLAN.md:392`).

The sequence is the new route's own phases 1 and 2. The only difference is
where the architecture ruling sits. I would place it at LJ-2.1, not at the
phase gate. I would keep D26's rule: a number alone does not change the
route; a measured benchmark plus a reuse map does
(`archive/dev/DECISIONS-archived.md:47`).

The harder question: is there a decision available on 2026-08-09 that beats
both the chosen route and the status quo? Yes. The phased deferral above
beats both. It beats the chosen route because it lands a trophy and a
measured benchmark before the architecture bet. It beats the status quo
because it keeps the two-tower option open until measured. Every piece of
it existed on 2026-08-09: D38's line one, the LJ-1.x rows, and DD5's
make-clear-first clause. Its cost is that phase 3 starts later. The chosen
route's cost is a trophy deferred and a benchmark still unmeasured.

I do not refute the two-tower bridge route. The record supports it as an
architecture. The record refutes the timing of the ruling, not the target.
I would refute the route if LJ-3.1's reuse map cannot close the gap that
T257 and T260 measured, or if the internalization wing measures far below
the two-tower route's price.

## 6. WHAT I AM NOT SURE OF

1. **Whether the owner had non-numeric reasons for the change.** The record
   shows the architecture argument. The fine-structure literature's J-native
   form was one of D18's non-line tie-breakers
   (`archive/dev/DECISIONS-archived.md:40`). A private reason would not
   appear here.
   Evidence that would close it: the owner's own note of 2026-08-09, if one
   exists outside the repository.

2. **Whether T257's weak point would have moved the band enough.** The
   probe never ran (`archive/dev/TASKS-archived.md:266`). The weak point is
   a fifth of the band (`dev/ledger.toml:141-148`). Evidence: run the T261
   probe and re-price the comparison.

3. **Whether the internalization GCH wing would have re-priced up again.**
   The L3.32 wing re-priced up on every survey-class term. The LJ-1.x wing
   over the delivered coding tower was never priced end to end. Evidence:
   LJ-1.1's recon with its widest unmeasured term named and gated.

4. **Whether DD4's repetition mechanism will hold.** The record shows a
   similar mechanism, the playbook citation, drifted for five days
   (`dev/rules.toml:1-10`). Repetition is the enforcement DD4 names. Its
   history is mixed. Evidence: time, and the audit of the first briefs
   written under DD4.

5. **Whether the archived J tower content ports cheaply.** The archive is
   reference, not a shortcut (`dev/PLAN.md:158`). Port rates were measured
   against the retiring tree's closure, not against the archive's files
   (`dev/LESSONS.md:1551-1563`). Evidence: LJ-3.4's per-module pricing,
   which the record has not yet produced.
