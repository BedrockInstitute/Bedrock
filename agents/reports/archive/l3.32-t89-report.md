### 2.2 [T84]'s decode design: the readings hold, the arity-4 shape needed two adjustments

T84's arity-4 formulas (limPr/limIn/limOut at the environment
`(y, a, f, x)`) were verified by hand and machine-checked at concrete
pairs in a new scratch probe (`/private/tmp/T89Design.agda`, exit 0):

| check | claim | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `limPr-sat` | at `(one, ∅, fSet, two)` reads "pr ∅ one ∈ fSet" | green |
| `limIn-sat` | at `(two, one, fSet, x)` reads "z ∈ y ⇒ ∃ p ∈ a, ∃ q ∈ f, q = pr p z" | green |
| `limOut-sat` | at `(two, one, fSet, x)` reads the converse | green |
| `hsndOf` / `hsndOf-deg` / `¬hsndOf-deg` | the uniform second-component atom `sndOf` reads "v = snd(q)" on non-degenerate and degenerate pairs, and rejects the wrong value | green |
| `exactDom-sat5` / `¬domForm-sat5` | the delivered `domForm` does NOT read `exactDom` | green (fourth defect) |

Two adjustments to T84's design were needed, both found by the D-10
derivation before building:

1. **`limOut`'s premise cannot be `fstLt`.** `fstLt k l` reads
   "fst(k) ∈ fst(l)" and requires `l` to be a pair (it binds a singleton
   member of `l`). In `limOut` the premise is "fst(q) ∈ a" with `a` the
   limit ordinal, which is not a pair; `fstLt q a` would be unsatisfiable
   (no singleton member of an ordinal), making the whole premise vacuous.
   The premise is read by the new atom `fstIn` ("the first component of q
   is a member of a").
2. **The bounded decomposition's reverse direction needs a case split on
   the pair's degeneracy.** T84's arity-4 environment is populated at the
   real tower by unbounded quantifiers; the bounded clause must decompose
   `p = pr a y` itself, and the second component `y` is read by `sndOf`,
   whose two branches (non-degenerate: `y ∈ y₂ ∧ y ∉ x₁`; degenerate:
   every member of `p` is a singleton and `y ∈ x₁`) require choosing a
   branch when constructing the satisfaction. The forward decode
   (satisfaction → `limitClause`) is unaffected: the existential witness
   `y` delivered by the formula *is* `snd(p)` by `sndOf`'s reading. The
   reverse (limitClause → satisfaction) must produce the witness `m ∈ p`
   and the `sndOf` branch, and the choice depends on whether `y = a`,
   which is not decidable in this hierarchy. The split is available from
   the `kpair` satisfaction's own disjunction (every member of `p` is
   either the singleton or a two-element set), but threading it through
   the decode is real additional machinery, priced in section 3.

So [T84]'s design held in its readings (verified), and its
implementation needed the `fstIn` correction and the degeneracy handling.
Neither invalidates the design; both are the kind of binder-level detail
this region has repeatedly hidden.

## 8. Walls by LESSONS class, and the proposed law

**Wall class: D-10, twice.**

1. **The pair-level satisfaction structure.** The `pair2-out` decode (the
   innermost atom of the pairhood/limit machinery) repeatedly failed to
   elaborate: the eliminator's pattern for the inner bounded-existential
   satisfaction was inferred against the membership `⟨ u ∈ˢ lookup k δ ⟩`
   (itself a truncation) rather than the pair `(membership × body)`, and
   the fix was not a proof issue but a structure-elaboration issue that
   consumed the decode attempt. The wall is the D-10 class in its sharpest
   form: a green shape (the formula and its Δ₀ witness) did not reveal the
   satisfaction structure the decode must eliminate; the decode attempt
   was the truth check, and it surfaced the structure.
2. **The bounded decomposition's degeneracy split.** The limit clause's
   reverse decode needs to construct the `sndOf` branch for a pair
   `p = pr a y`, and the correct branch depends on whether `y = a`, which
   is not decidable in this hierarchy. The split is constructively
   available from the `kpair` satisfaction's own disjunction, but threading
   it is new machinery, priced above.

**Proposed law (D-10 family; next free D id):** *a bounded clause that
decomposes a pairing structure inherits the degenerate-pair case split in
its reverse decode, and a two-way decode against a statement quantified
over all pairs must supply that split constructively or restate the
clause.* Measurement: the delivered `limitClause`
([LevelFormula.lagda.md:152-155](/Users/alsg/Agentic/Bedrock/src/L/LevelFormula.lagda.md:152))
quantifies all `(a, y)`; the bounded replacement's reverse direction
cannot be built without either deciding `y = a` or threading the `kpair`
disjunction; the forward direction is unaffected because the formula's
witness pins the second component. Enforcement point: when a bounded
clause is written over Kuratowski pairs, check the reverse decode at a
degenerate pair before pricing it.

**No performance wall.** The added content is free to check (203.3 s cold,
unchanged); the pair2-out wall is an elaboration-structure issue, not a
conversion wall.
