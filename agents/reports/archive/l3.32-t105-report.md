## 1. Does the diagnosis survive? No.

**The count recomputes correctly, and the inference from citation to
application does not survive contact with the tree.**

  09:41, t98 09:42, t99 10:18, t100 10:24, t101 11:04, t102 11:51, t103
  12:23, t104 12:26).
- The citation frequency table: recomputed on all 113 briefs, the top ten

  D-1 31, R-37 29. The +1s against the memo's table (D-10 141, R-35 58,

- Dates: `Rule 1` to `Rule 20` landed 2026-08-01 in `e8cb18c`; P-i landed

  entries + 16 numbered Rules, plus 23 PLAN decisions = 128.

### 1.2 The "only earlier one" is dated inside the era, not before it

The memo says: "The only earlier one predates the rud route"
(`dev/memos/L3.32-context-layering.md:63-65`). The earlier one is
`_build/briefs/t22-w3seal-brief.md`, an L3.32-T22 brief (the W3 sealing
design probe), dated 2026-08-04 21:29, with its report dated 2026-08-04
21:56. That date sits between Bridge's first commit (Aug 3) and
SquareLaw/Condensation (Aug 5), inside the very era the memo's own table
1.2 defines. It is not a pre-era brief. It cites P-i three times
(`t22-w3seal-brief.md:25-27`: "dev/LESSONS.md (BINDING and the point of the
exercise: R-35 ..., R-36 ..., R-38 ..., I-5 ..., P-i layer caps and
huge-argument rules, D-16 ...)"). So under the memo's own definition of
"Rule N or P-i", the playbook WAS cited in the era, once, three times, and
the probe that cited it succeeded. The claim "for the entire rud-route era,
the internalization playbook was cited essentially zero times"
(`L3.32-context-layering.md:67-68`) is false on its own counting rule.

### 1.3 Low citation is not non-application, and the tree proves it

`[T100]` swept everything outside the three big theorems against the
playbook, rule by rule, and found exactly ONE live violation in the whole
sweep (`_build/l3.32-t100-report.md:94`; "No other rule violation with a
measured anchor exists in the sweep" at `:83`; the clean-bill summary in
`dev/PLAN.md` row L3.32-F3.1), and that one
(`right-spec` unsealed at `src/L/Rud/Images.lagda.md:268-269`) was refuted
by measurement: the seal buys zero, 52.9 to 53.2 s (`dev/PLAN.md`, row
L3.32-F3.1). The playbook is followed in 58 measured modules whose briefs
did not cite it. Citation is therefore neither necessary for application
(T100) nor sufficient for correctness (T88's confounded experiment, cited
in the memo's own "What this does NOT do" at
`L3.32-context-layering.md:140-146`). The memo never mentions T100 at all:
`rg "T100|sweep" dev/memos/L3.32-context-layering.md` finds nothing. The
strongest counter-evidence is not addressed.

### 1.4 The briefs that built the three modules were the thinnest citers

The memo's section 1.3 claims "The build briefs are not lax. Each names
five to nine entries, and they do it every time" and shows t59, t69, t80,
t82. Those four recompute correctly (I verified their exact ID sets). But
they are not the briefs that built the three expensive modules:

- `_build/briefs/t31-squarelaw-brief.md` (SquareLaw builder) cites exactly
  one LESSONS entry: R-38.
- `_build/briefs/t33-condense-brief.md` (Condensation builder) cites exactly
  one: D-10.
- `_build/briefs/t35-reduce-brief.md` (Bridge's reshaped reduction) cites
  exactly two: D-8, C-10.

So the modules at issue were NOT built under "the same ten IDs cited
hundreds of times". They were built under near-empty citation. The
"stale habit" story does not fit the briefs that matter; a thin brief and
an unread rule are different failures, and the memo conflates them.

## 2. For each of the three expensive modules: name the rule that would have prevented it

**None of the three defects is a case of "the rule existed, was never
cited, and citation would have stopped it." For two of the three, the
rule that covers the defect did not exist when the module was written.
The gap is missing rules, not unread ones.**

### 2.1 Bridge: the covering law is P-k, written 2026-08-06, after the fix

Bridge's defect is the read lemma stated one layer early: `γ-compute`'s
stated type ended at the tower body rather than at `+ω (U α)`, so each
`subst` consumer re-normalized the tower. The rule that names this is P-k,
"A read lemma is stated where its consumers use it, not where its proof
ends" (`dev/LESSONS.md:2051`), added in commit `f76c877` on 2026-08-06,
after the defect was found and fixed. P-k's own text is decisive:
"Why this needs its own entry when R-36 and R-38 already exist. Those two
say seal an unfolding read lemma, and seal it at birth. Both were followed
here: `γ-compute` WAS sealed. The seal was in the right place and the
statement was in the wrong place, which neither entry reaches"
(`dev/LESSONS.md:2070-2072`). So the pre-existing playbook's sealing rules
were followed and could not have stopped Bridge. No rule that existed on
Aug 3 would have prevented it. The preventing rule was written on the day
of this review.

### 2.2 SquareLaw: the covering law is P-l, written 2026-08-06, and the
pre-existing discipline failed by measurement

SquareLaw's cost is the bodies passing four concrete memberships at the
tower position; moving them into module parameters removed the term
entirely, 374.3 s to 82 ms (`dev/LESSONS.md:2013`, T102's refutation of
T88). The rule that covers this class is P-l, "A statement may be ABOUT a
concrete stage without dragging that stage's PRESENTATION into its type"
(`dev/LESSONS.md:1965`), added in commit `b4a4dd7` on 2026-08-06.

Could a pre-existing rule have done it? The pre-existing discipline was
tried and measured: "the abstract-carrier discipline ... transplanted to
`SquareLaw`'s chase ... heap exhausted at `-M8g`, twice"
(`dev/LESSONS.md:1994-1996`, P-l's own transplant table). The fix that
worked, abstracting the SOURCE rather than the target, is P-l's content,
written after the measurement. And the t31 brief DID cite R-38, the
playbook's sealing law, and said LESSONS is binding
(`t31-squarelaw-brief.md:24-26`), and the module still became the tree's
most expensive chapter. Citation happened, and it did not prevent the
defect, because the cited rule was the wrong one and the right one did not
exist.

### 2.3 Condensation: the closest rule existed (P-i [E]) but its cure was
refused as export-breaking even after full measurement

Condensation's cost is body-bound: two assembly definitions elaborate
delivered machinery at the concrete formula `LsetGraphAt {2} zero (suc
zero)`; gutting the body moves the cost without changing the total
(`_build/l3.32-t96-report.md:11-14`). The closest pre-existing rule is
P-i's [E] "Reshape the obligation", which says the only cure is changing
the upstream interface so the obligation never applies the heavy operator
to the deep term (`dev/LESSONS.md:271-279`), present since 2026-08-02
(`d1be64a`). This is the one module where a rule that names the cure
existed at build time.

But citing it would not have produced a cheap module. T96 measured the
bill of materials: about 54 s is the delivered `Lset-only` application,
"the chapter's actual content, with no cheaper body of the same type", and
the ~77 s and ~56 s removals each change an exported statement or the
`Crossing` module signature (`_build/l3.32-t96-report.md:96-132`; the
removal table at `:124-131`; "No export-preserving combination exists:
every lever touches an exported statement" at `:132`). The verdict is "no
cheap export-preserving fix exists" and the module is a priced refusal
under D30. So even today, with P-i [E] in the corpus and fully measured,
the campaign chose to keep the cost rather than change exports. The gap
is a refused interface decision, not a missing citation.

**Section 2 verdict:** the proposal's premise, "the rules existed before
the code that needed them", is true for Rules 1-20 and P-i and false for
the rules that actually cover these defects. P-k and P-l both landed on
2026-08-06. The corpus could not have prevented Bridge or SquareLaw by
any amount of citation, and Condensation's cure is a decision the campaign
already declined.

## 3. The mechanism's defects, worst first

### 3.1 Clause 2 does not do what the proposal says it does

The load-bearing claim is: "on 2026-08-01 sixteen Rules entered the corpus
and entered no bundle, so nothing ever cited them. Under this check, that
commit would not have passed the gate" (`L3.32-context-layering.md:133-135`).

The check as specified refuses "a `dev/LESSONS.md` entry that belongs to no
bundle and has no trigger term" (`:129-130`). Two escape routes exist, and
both are the natural moves of the very author whose memory the diagnosis
blames:

1. **Trigger-term escape.** Every Rule has an obvious trigger (`opaque`,
   `subst`, `with`, `PT.rec` are all in the memo's own Layer 2 example,
   `:114-116`). Adding a trigger term is a metadata chore that does not
   make anyone read or cite the rule. The 08-01 commit passes with sixteen
   trigger rows and zero bundles.
2. **Bundle bloat escape.** The author assigns all sixteen Rules to
   `build`, which already carries 13 IDs in the memo's own example
   (`:104-108`). The mandatory set becomes the corpus again. The proposal
   has no cap on bundle size, no eviction rule, and no review of bundle
   membership when an entry is added. Bundle bloat is named as a suspicion
   in the dispatch; it is not defended against in the memo.

So the statement "that commit would not have passed the gate" is false as
written: the gate passes on metadata presence, and the metadata is written
by the same party whose citation habit is the alleged failure.

### 3.2 The checker enforces citation, and the corpus's own evidence says
citation is not application

Clause 1 compares "the cited IDs" against the bundle
(`L3.32-context-layering.md:127-128`). The memo's own "What this does NOT
do" concedes the mechanism "does not help when a rule is cited and
misapplied, which is what happened with `[T88]`'s confounded experiment and
with the R-38 anchor that measured zero" (`:140-146`). T88 is a case where
the rule WAS read and applied and still produced a wrong diagnosis; the
R-38 anchor is a case where the rule's own text said "sealing only moves
the cost" (`dev/LESSONS.md:835`) and the estimate contradicted the rule it
cited. A checker that fails a brief for not listing an ID would have passed
all three of the module briefs in section 2.1-2.3 (t31 cited R-38, t33
cited D-10, t35 cited D-8 and C-10) and changed nothing.

### 3.3 The enforcement point is not in the tree

Clause 1 runs in `dispatch.py check <brief>` (`:126-127`). `dispatch.py`
does not exist under `scripts/`: `find . -name dispatch.py` returns only
`./.claude/skills/codex-dispatch/dispatch.py`, and `ls scripts/` has no
such file. `ORCHESTRATION.md:195` already references `dispatch.py
gate-ready` as though it were standard tooling. The proposal does not say
`check-rules.py` joins `make check` or the pre-commit hook, and the
constraint on this project's agents is never to touch `.claude/`. A gate
that lives outside the committed tree is exactly the kind of enforcement
that gets forgotten, which is the failure mode the proposal is supposed to
fix. If it is not in `make check` or the hook, "refuse, do not warn" has no
enforcement point (AGENTS.md: "A rule with no enforcement point is a
wish").

### 3.4 The example `rules.toml` already contains an ID that does not exist

The memo's `[bundle.rewrite]` example carries `"D-19-is-not-quotable"`
(`L3.32-context-layering.md:109`). The corpus has `### D-19. Price a port
against the retiring tree's transitive closure, and split both directions`
(`dev/LESSONS.md:1520`); there is no entry with the ID
`D-19-is-not-quotable` anywhere. That ID is an ORCHESTRATION paraphrase
("D-19's port rates must not be quoted", `ORCHESTRATION.md:111`). The
proposal's own artifact demonstrates the exact drift it claims to prevent:
an unvalidated ID, hand-typed into a second home. There is no stated check
that every `rules.toml` ID resolves to a LESSONS heading, and no procedure
for rename, merge, or withdrawal. The withdrawal case is live: P-l's
statement-bound category was WITHDRAWN today (`dev/LESSONS.md:2013`), so any
one-line statement cached for P-l in `rules.toml` is already stale.

### 3.5 `rules.toml` and `CORE.md` violate the one-canonical-home rule

AGENTS.md: "Every rule has ONE canonical home" and "Nothing is canonical in
two places." The proposal splits a rule's text (LESSONS.md) from its
routing (`rules.toml`) and adds a third artifact, `CORE.md`, whose
candidates include rules that are not LESSONS entries at all: "the
both-dimensions rule" lives in `ORCHESTRATION.md:99`, and
"evidence-is-file:line" lives in `AGENTS.md:142`. If `CORE.md` carries
their text, those rules now have two or three homes. The 1,200-word cap
(`L3.32-context-layering.md:90`) has no admission test, no eviction
procedure, and no decision maker: "a rule that cannot displace an existing
one does not belong" names a displacement test that nobody is specified to
run. A cap with no eviction procedure is a wish.

### 3.6 Self-declared `kind:` is a real hole, and the fix is mechanical

The orchestrator writes the brief AND declares its `kind:`
(`L3.32-context-layering.md:127`), so it can pick the kind with the
smallest bundle (the memo's own bundles differ by 4 to 13 IDs). The hole is
real. It is also cheap to close: derive the kind from the write territory
in the same brief. A brief whose SCOPE (write) names a master under `src/`
is a build; one that names `src/Probe*.agda` is a probe; one that names
only `_build/` is a recon. That derivation is mechanical and can be the
checker's input instead of the author's claim.

### 3.7 The recurring cost is understated

"the recurring cost is one `rules.py` call per brief" (`:152`) omits: a
`kind:` judgment per brief; a bundle-membership review whenever the corpus
changes (today: P-l withdrawn, P-k and P-l added, C-23 added); the
maintenance of 128 rows of metadata plus trigger terms; and the resolution
of what counts as "cited" (a scope-list mention is not a reading, and the
memo's own count treats any mention as a citation). The layer-1 build
bundle in the example already has 13 mandatory IDs, more than any current
brief cites (5-9), so "make the core small" becomes "make the mandatory
list larger".

## 4. Checkers versus routing

**The mechanically checkable fraction is small, but it contains the one
failure class that actually mattered, and that class is already covered.**

The 128 rules are mostly performance and elaboration laws (P series, Rules
1-20, R series): they say what the elaborator does to a proof shape, which
no static linter can see. What can be checked today, or plausibly checked,
is the part that is either syntactic or measured:

- Cost (the actual defect class): `scripts/check-timing.py` fails a module
  for being expensive, as a regression against its own baseline and as a
  tree share above 2 percent requiring a per-definition profile. It was
  created 2026-08-06 (`a9db012`), the same day as the freeze, and it is the
  only gate in the repository that can fail a module for being expensive
  (`ORCHESTRATION.md:69-75`, `scripts/check-timing.py:3-12`). Had it existed
  on Aug 3-5, it would have flagged all three modules.
- Structure: `check-tree.py` (closure, archive, shared-CJK, SPDX, retiring,
  module-body), `check-probes.py` (D-1 lifecycle), `check-glossary.py`,
  `ledger.py`, `lint-agda.py`, `lint-prose.py`, `reuse lint`.
- Plausibly addable with modest effort: Rule 10 (no `with` on membership
  splits), I-3 (bindings carry type signatures inside opaque blocks),
  C-2, C-10, C-19, C-12 as dispatch-time checks. That is on the order of
  10-15 percent of the corpus.

The remaining ~85 percent cannot be routed into usefulness either: T88 and
the R-38 anchor prove a cited, applied rule can still be misapplied, and
T100 proves the playbook is followed where never cited. The one defect the
corpus genuinely could not catch, cost, is caught by `check-timing.py`, and
the proposal never mentions it: `rg "check-timing|timing" 
dev/memos/L3.32-context-layering.md` finds nothing. A routing layer adds
metadata presence; a checker adds detection. The checker dominates where
it exists, and the proposal proposes no new checker at all.

## 5. Recommendation: do not implement as proposed

The diagnosis fails on its own evidence: the counts are right, but the
preventing rules for Bridge and SquareLaw (P-k, P-l) were written on
2026-08-06, after the defects; T100 shows the playbook is applied where
never cited; the one in-era citation (t22-w3seal) succeeded and did not
prevent the later failures; and the briefs that built the three modules
were the thinnest citers in the corpus. The mechanism's load-bearing
clause is escapable by trigger terms or bundle bloat, its enforcement
point is not in the tree, its own example bundle contains a non-existent
rule ID, and it enforces citation, which the corpus's evidence says is not
application.

What the proposal gets right, and what is worth keeping, is small and
cheap:

- The newly written laws (P-k, P-l, P-l's body-bound row) are the actual
  gap, and they now exist. The cheapest correct next step is to put them
  into the standing brief clauses (the ORCHESTRATION "SCOPE (read)... name
  the hot entries" discipline, `ORCHESTRATION.md:52-54`) so the habit
  refreshes immediately, without a routing layer.
- If any routing is built, it must cap bundle size, derive `kind:` from the
  write territory, validate every `rules.toml` ID against LESSONS headings,
  and live in `make check` or the pre-commit hook, not in `.claude/`.
- The measurement gate is the part that dominates: extend
  `scripts/check-timing.py` coverage and keep the seconds clause in every
  brief (`ORCHESTRATION.md:99`). That is the answer to the one defect the
  corpus could not catch.

Do not implement the memo's layer-0/1/2 machinery as specified. The
report that says "the proposal looks reasonable" would have failed; this
one kills the load-bearing claim with evidence: the rules that would have
prevented the defects did not exist when the defects were written, and the
one earlier in-era citation did not prevent them either.
