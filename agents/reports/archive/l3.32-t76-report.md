# [L3.32-T76] Fix `sndIn`, and find out what was resting on the wrong reading

**Verdict, compressed.** The T75 correction is right: two index changes
inside `sndIn` give the second-component reading, and everything still
typechecks. The audit adds two findings T75 did not carry: (1) the corrected
`snd∈Snd` asserts **equality** of second components (`snd(k) = snd(l)`), not
the membership the limit clause needs, so `limOut` still does not read the
delivered inclusion; and (2) `limIn` has its own binder error (its second
binder ranges over the singleton member, not the pair member; the value
inclusion is vacuous), machine-checked unsatisfiable at a concrete pair. The
fix therefore repairs exactly the range read `Rg`; it does not repair the
limit clause, and D5 is closer by one atom, not unblocked.

## 1. My own derivation of the corrected reading (D-10 first)

Re-derived independently of T70/T75, from the satisfaction clause

and the de Bruijn convention (each binder prepends its witness, so the bound
variable sits at index 0, [Syntax.lagda.md:96-100](/Users/alsg/Agentic/Bedrock/src/FOL/Syntax.lagda.md)).

`sndIn ku kw` ([Condensation.lagda.md:382-387](/Users/alsg/Agentic/Bedrock/src/L/Condensation.lagda.md),
with the fix applied):

```text
∃̇∈ (var kw)            -- x₁ ∈ kw, pinned singleton by sgl f0
  ( sgl f0
  ∧̇ ∃̇∈ (var (suc kw))  -- y₂ ∈ kw, pinned two-element by pair2 f0
      ( pair2 f0
      ∧̇ ∀̇∈ (var (suc zero)) (var f0 ∈̇ var (suc zero))   -- (i) x₁ ⊆ y₂
      ∧̇ var (suc (suc ku)) ∈̇ var f0                      -- (ii) ku ∈ y₂
      ∧̇ ∀̇∈ (var (suc zero)) (¬̇ (var f0 ≐ var (suc (suc (suc ku)))))  -- (iii)
      ) )
```

Index arithmetic (each binder shifts the original env by one):

- Under binder 1 the original `ku`, `kw` sit at `suc ku`, `suc kw`; under
  binder 2 at `suc (suc ku)`, `suc (suc kw)`; under the third binder at
  `suc (suc (suc ku))`.
- Binder 2's term `var (suc kw)` is the original `kw` again (not the
  first-bound `x₁`), so `y₂` is a member of the pair `kw`. The fresh
  witnesses sit at the small fixed indices: `var zero` = `y₂`,
  `var (suc zero)` = `x₁`.
- Conjunct (ii), `var (suc (suc ku)) ∈̇ var f0`: the original `ku` is a member
  of the pair member `y₂` (the OLD code targeted `var (suc zero)` = `x₁`, the
  singleton member, reading `ku = a`, the first component).
- Conjunct (iii), `∀̇∈ (var (suc zero)) (...)`: ranges over the members of
  `x₁` (the OLD code ranged over `var (suc (suc kw))` = the whole pair `kw`);
  the body excludes `ku = a`, where `a` is the element of the singleton
  member.

So the corrected formula reads: `ku ∈ y₂ ∧ ku ≠ a` with `y₂ = ⁅ a , b ⁆`
(a two-element set) and `x₁ = ⁅ a ⁆`, which forces `ku = b`, the second
component of the Kuratowski pair `kw`. This is the comment's reading
([Condensation.lagda.md:381](/Users/alsg/Agentic/Bedrock/src/L/Condensation.lagda.md))
and the reading every use site needs. **D-10 verdict: T75's two changes are
correct; I re-derived them without inheriting the report's arithmetic.**

## 2. What broke, if anything, and what the old reading was silently supplying

**What the old reading was silently supplying:** a range read that was a
domain read. `Rg` ([Condensation.lagda.md:534-535](/Users/alsg/Agentic/Bedrock/src/L/Condensation.lagda.md))
composed the buggy `sndIn`, so the story's range clause read "`x` is a FIRST
component of a pair in `f`", while the delivered range read is "`x` is the
second component", `x ∈ran f = ∥ Σ[ a ] ⟨ pr a x ∈ˢ f ⟩ ∥₁`
([InitialSegment.lagda.md:83-85](/Users/alsg/Agentic/Bedrock/src/L/InitialSegment.lagda.md)),
in object form at
([LevelFormula.lagda.md:270-272](/Users/alsg/Agentic/Bedrock/src/L/LevelFormula.lagda.md)).
Everything built on the story (`σᴹ` :589, `σL` :644, the transports, the
Levy certificates) carried that wrong content. The delivered proofs never
depended on the semantic content of the formulas (they are syntax and
transport certificates), which is exactly why nothing broke and why the
defect survived three dispatches (section 6).

**Two findings the fix does NOT repair (new, machine-checked):**

1. **`limIn` has its own binder error.** The second binder of `limIn`
   ([Condensation.lagda.md:503-510](/Users/alsg/Agentic/Bedrock/src/L/Condensation.lagda.md))
   is `∃̇∈ (var zero)` after the first binder over `p`, so it ranges over the
   SINGLETON member `x₁`, forcing the pair2 witness to be a member of a
   singleton (`∅` or `one` for `p = pr ∅ one`); neither is a two-element set.
   The rest of the clause is vacuous: the inner `∀̇∈ (var (suc (suc zero)))`
   ranges over `x₁` again, so its premise is always false. The intended
   reading ("every member of the value `y` is a value below `a`",
   matching the delivered `limIn` at
   [LevelFormula.lagda.md:245-249](/Users/alsg/Agentic/Bedrock/src/L/LevelFormula.lagda.md))
   needs the pair member, then the value's members. Machine-checked:
   `limIn-unsat` (claim F) refutes the CURRENT formula at
   `(pr ∅ one, f, x)` for every `f x` in the scratch probe.
2. **The corrected `snd∈Snd` reads equality, not membership.** `snd∈Snd k l`
   ([Condensation.lagda.md:396-401](/Users/alsg/Agentic/Bedrock/src/L/Condensation.lagda.md))
   is "∃ z ∈ y₂(k): z ∉ x₁(k) ∧ sndIn z l". The first part forces `z =
   snd(k)`; the corrected `sndIn z l` asserts `z = snd(l)`. So `snd∈Snd k l`
   asserts `snd(k) = snd(l)` (machine-checked: `sndIn-ref-q` shows `sndIn`
   rejects `one` for `pr ∅ two` even though `one ∈ two`; `snd∈Snd-sat` shows
   the equal case satisfies it). `limOut`
   ([Condensation.lagda.md:521-522](/Users/alsg/Agentic/Bedrock/src/L/Condensation.lagda.md))
   therefore reads "all values below `a` equal the value at `a`", whereas the
   delivered second inclusion needs "values below `a` are members of `y`"
   (`limitClause`, [LevelFormula.lagda.md:152-155](/Users/alsg/Agentic/Bedrock/src/L/LevelFormula.lagda.md)).
   The current `snd∈Snd` formula cannot express "z ∈ snd(l)" at all.

## 4. The D5 answer

**Closer by exactly one atom; not unblocked.** D5 stopped on the class-carrier
equivalence `σL ⟷ LsetGraphAt` at both readings, blocked on the satisfaction
decodes that reconcile the structural clauses with the delivered description
(T70 report, section 2 item 3 and section 5). One named component of that wall was the
range atom: "the delivered range atom is semantically mis-indiced, so the
structural `Rg` does not even mean `x ∈ ran f` at the story's own reading"
(T70 report, section 5). That component is now gone: after the fix, `Rg`
reads `x = snd(p)` for a pair `p ∈ f`, the range read, aligning the story's
range clause with the delivered range read at the story's own `(f, x)`
reading. The equivalence's range direction is no longer impossible on
content grounds.

The obstacle does not dissolve, for three reasons, two inherited and one new:

1. **Environment convention (inherited, untouched).** The story speaks at
   `(f, x)` (graph at 0, read member at 1); the delivered description speaks
   at `(v, b)` (value at 0, ordinal index at 1). A per-environment
   bi-implication between them is false, not hard (T70 report, section 5);
   the fix does not touch this.
2. **The other clauses (inherited, untouched).** `pairForm`, `singleForm`,
   `zeroForm`, `domForm` still need their decodes against `pairhood`,
   `singleValued`, `zeroClause`, `domOrd`.
3. **The limit clause (new, now precise).** The structural `limIn ∧̇ limOut`
   do not read the delivered `limitClause` even after the fix: `limIn`'s
   second binder ranges over the singleton member and its body is vacuous
   (machine-checked unsatisfiable at a concrete pair), and `limOut`'s
   `snd∈Snd` asserts equality of second components instead of membership in
   the value. These are index-level defects, not decode-level: the equivalence
   row (c) must REWRITE `limIn`/`limOut`'s binder structure, then decode the
   rewritten clauses; decoding the current clauses cannot reach `limitClause`.

So: D5 is closer by the range atom (one of the six approximation clauses now
matches the description at the story's own reading), and the row (c) price
does not shrink overall, because the limit clause is now known to carry
index-level repair work beyond the sndIn fix.
