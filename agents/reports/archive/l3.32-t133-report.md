   Formula ⟪ fst A ⟫ 1 → S) (keyʟ : Formula S 1 → S) (keyBridge : (ψ :

   both : ...)`
   ([TowerGraph.lagda.md:215-245](/Users/alsg/Agentic/Bedrock/src/L/TowerGraph.lagda.md:215-245)).
   These are the per-conjunct and semantic readings the arm walks on; their
   types mention the fresh description, which is why they sit after it.

Why two frame levels rather than one: the description text (`envOneAt`,
`isCodeAt`, `DefinesAt`, `DefBody`, `DefAt`) is written from the text
parameters, and the reading parameters' types mention the fresh description,
so the readings must be declared after the text. Agda module parameters are
all declared before the body, hence the split: `Frame` holds the texts and
the description, `Readings` holds the semantic readings and the arm. The
carrier is its own level because its type `S` exists only after the opens,
which is the same reason `StepStory` puts the layer telescope after its
header (T126 report section 2).

Why generic at all (D29, P-h): Build A's whole point is one fresh home
instantiated by the remaining eleven arms; the arm is proved once against the
interface, and the instantiators supply the cone's content at the interface's
types. A carrier-fixed or cone-fixed write would make every later arm re-derive
the same statements at its own carrier.

When the table block instantiates the frame, the arm's `fill`/`read`/
`DefAt-in`/`DefAt-out` and the two-slot closure `DefAt₂-in/out` are available
in the instantiated module, and the step/approx block can consume `DefAt₂`

`[T129]` section 1.1).

**Effect on `[T132]`'s 1,630-1,940: neutral on lines, strictly cheaper on
check seconds.** The tail's fresh-compressed size estimate (620-930, the
table/slot/sound/unique re-derivation) is unchanged: the frame does not write
the tail, it pins its contract. What changes is where the tail's conversions
run. `[T132]` measured the arm at 21.7 s cold with the cone imported
transparently, 60 percent of it in two truncation branches (`read.step`
6,838 ms, `fill` 6,064 ms) and a third I-4 spot (`qv` 5,413 ms), because the
imported bodies re-normalized at each application. With the frame abstract,
the same arm checks at 2.72 s module-cold (profile total 2,470 ms), largest
definition 141 ms, and `qv` is 15 ms (section 5). The tail's seconds are not
free: they are paid once in the instantiating block's own check, where P-k's
"normalize exactly once" says they belong, instead of at every arm's
application. For eleven arms sharing one frame, that is the difference
between roughly 11 x 20 s of tail re-normalization and one tail-block check
plus eleven ~1-3 s arm checks; the frame also forces the tail's statements to
the exact equation-reached, carrier-relative, arity-1 shapes the arm applies
(the I-4/I-5 discipline made structural, so a later block cannot deliver a
form that re-normalizes per consumer). The caveat, stated plainly: the
instantiating blocks should instantiate the frame in one module (or seal the
parameters, R-36/R-38), so the conversions are not re-paid per consumer.

Net: not cheaper in the adequacy's line count (the tail's 620-930 estimate
stands), but the frame absorbs `[T132]`'s counted small frame (~123 lines,
`[T132]` section 4) into this block's 304 rather than re-counting it, and it
changes the adequacy's check-time class from "seconds per arm per application"
to "once in the table block".
