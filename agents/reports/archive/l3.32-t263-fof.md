# [L3.32-T263] Are all sixteen Fof graphs Δ₀ at the delivered specs?

**First line: No. At the delivered formulas, none of the sixteen operation
graphs is Δ₀. All sixteen fail on one shared unbounded quantifier. Nine
fail again inside their membership bodies: F3, F4, F6, F8, F10, F11, F12,
F13, F14.**

## 1. The verdict

The delivered graph of every operation is `graphOf i = eqFrame f2 (memOf i)`
(`src/L/Rud/StepGraph.lagda.md:1729`). The equality frame is

```
eqFrame k M = (∀̇∈ (var k) M) ∧̇ (∀̇ (M ⇒̇ (var f0 ∈̇ var (suc k))))
```

(`src/L/Rud/StepGraph.lagda.md:189-190`). At the three free variables
`b, a, y`, the graph reads

`(∀ x ∈ y. memOf i (x, b, a, y)) ∧ (∀ x. memOf i (x, b, a, y) → x ∈ y)`.

The second conjunct contains `∀̇`. The Δ₀ witness has no constructor for
`∀̇` or `∃̇` (`src/FOL/LevyHierarchy.lagda.md:47-57`). The witness stops at
`δ-∀∈` and `δ-∃∈`. Therefore `graphOf i` has no Δ₀ witness for any `i`.

The semantics of `∀̇` is a universal over the whole domain of the structure
(`src/FOL/Semantics.lagda.md:100-103`). At a stage carrier, that domain is
the stage itself (`src/L/Rud/StepGraph.lagda.md:78-80`). The frame does not
bind the coverage quantifier by the value `y` or by any argument.

The delivered chapter says this plainly: "Nothing below is Δ₀"
(`src/L/Rud/StepGraph.lagda.md:80`).

### The quantifier audit, one row at a time

The environment of a body is `z, b, a, y` (`src/L/Rud/StepGraph.lagda.md:696-699`).
The body says "z is a member of the operation value at a and b".

**F0, mem0** (`:656`): `(var f0 ≐ var f2) ∨̇ (var f0 ≐ var f1)`, that is
`z ≐ a ∨ z ≐ b`. No quantifiers. Body Δ₀. Graph fails on the frame only.

**F1, mem1** (`:657`): `(var f0 ∈̇ var f2) ∧̇ (¬̇ (var f0 ∈̇ var f1))`, that

**F2, mem2** (`:658`): `∃̇∈ (var f2) (∃̇∈ (var f2) (PK.prAt f2 f1 f0))`.
The two bounded existentials range over `a` and `b`. `prAt` uses only
bounded quantifiers, all over its pair argument (`src/L/PairAtoms.lagda.md:158-161`).
Body Δ₀.

**F3, mem3** (`:659`): `∃̇∈ (var f1) tuple3L0`. The scaffolding is
`tuple3L0 = ∃̇ tuple3L1`, `tuple3L1 = ∃̇ tuple3L2`,
`tuple3L2 = ∃̇∈ (var f5) tuple3L3`, `tuple3L3 = ∃̇ tuple3L4`
(`:629-642`). The body has three unbounded `∃̇` and two bounded `∃̇∈`

**F4, mem4** (`:660`): the mirror of mem3 through `tuple4L0` (`:630-643`).

**F5, mem5** (`:661`): `∃̇∈ (var f2) (var f1 ∈̇ var f0)`, that is

**F7, mem7** (`:663`): two bounded `∃̇∈` over `a`, with `prAt` bounded.

**F8, mem8** (`:664`): `∃̇∈ (var f1) (eqFrame f1 sliceMem)`, that is

## 3. Departure from the classical Δ₀ list

The delivered sets match the classical rudimentary list. F0 is the pair,
F1 the difference, F2 the product, F3 the middle insertion, F4 the right
append, F5 the union, F6 the domain, F7 the membership relation, F8 the
image collection, F9 the ordered pair, F10 the slice, F11-F14 the four
component shuffles, F15 the intersection with the slot set. The classical
basis is chosen so each graph is Δ₀.

The delivered formulas depart from the classical presentation in three
ways. Each departure is a difference of shape, not a difference of set.

First, the frame. The classical graph states the coverage direction with
assertions or quantifiers bounded by the arguments and the value. The
delivered frame states it as one universal over the whole domain. This
difference costs boundedness. It fails every delivered graph.

Second, the unbounded existentials. The classical graphs bind every witness
by the arguments or the value. F3, F4 and F6 bind pair components by the
double union of the container. F10 binds the pair by the double union of
`x`. F8 binds the slice equality by the double union of `x`. F11-F14 bind
the components by the double union of `y`. The delivered bodies use `∃̇`
for these witnesses. This difference costs boundedness in the delivered
formulas.

Third, the F15 constant. The delivered body names `A` through `con mA`.
The classical graph treats membership in `A` as an atom. In the
parameter-free axis, the slot `A` must be a free variable. This difference
does not cost boundedness. It costs the constant-free property.

The classical result does not transfer (P-l). The delivered specs are
meta-level membership characterizations. They state the sets, not bounded
object formulas. Examples are `F2-RHS`, `F3-RHS`, `F4-RHS`, `F6-RHS`
(`src/L/Rud/Ops.lagda.md:213-220, 286-293, 375-382, 506-512`). No delivered
text states a bounded object graph for any operation.

## 4. What this does to T259's design

The pre-gate fails as stated. T259 expected all sixteen to pass. None passes
at the delivered formulas.

The failure is at the level of formulas, not sets. No operation's set forces
an unbounded quantifier. Each set has a classical Δ₀ graph.

T259 cannot fill `Cl` by quoting the delivered `graphOf` formulas. It must
write sixteen fresh bounded clauses. The clauses need bounding terms the
delivered layer does not provide. F3, F4, F6, F8, F10, F11-F14 need the
double union of an argument. F15 needs the slot `A` as a variable. The
parameter-free axis has no constants (`src/L/Condensation.lagda.md:309-315`).

The Σ₁ claim of T259 line 2 survives only if the fresh clauses land with Δ₀
certificates and their decodes work at the parameter-free axis. That work is
new and unmeasured. The delivered carrier-indexed decodes do not supply it.

The stop-line of T259 section 6, "any unbounded quantifier forced into a
graph", is not triggered at the set level. The brief's own check, the
delivered formulas, fails on every graph. The report is a clean FAIL of the
delivered-formula check and a conditional pass of the set-level check.
