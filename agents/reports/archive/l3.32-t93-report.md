# L3.32-T93: find the next Bridge by reading

Status: COMPLETE. Read-only static audit of the surviving trunk (every
surviving module listed in `src/Everything.lagda.md`'s import closure, minus
`L.Rud.Bridge`, `L.Ordinal.SquareLaw`, `L.Condensation` and the D18 retiring
set). The only write is this report; the skeleton was created before the
audit and filled as answers landed (C-22). No Agda was run.

## 1. The ranked candidate list

**The headline finding: no definition in the surviving trunk satisfies all
four conditions of the Bridge checklist.** I read every tower read lemma, the
concrete-position statements, and the subst-heavy modules. In every case
where a lemma's stated type ends at a tower body or a union, every consumer
writes its proof against exactly that body, so there is no one-layer-too-early
endpoint and no repeated conversion. The list below is therefore not a list
of Bridges; it is the ranked list of places a freed profiling slot should
look first, each with the shape it matches and the number of consumers.

1. **L.Rud.Images: the `right-spec` invocation sites** (shape 3, the R-38
   class, with a measured anchor). `right-spec` (src/L/Rud/Images.lagda.md:268)
   is the transparent kit operation R5a measured at **25.7 s per invocation
   even at variable arguments**, 400x its sibling `left-spec` (64 ms)
   (`_build/r5a-report.md:468-481`). The surviving trunk still invokes
   `right-spec` at **four direct sites**: three inside Images itself,
   `F10-spec`'s backward direction at src/L/Rud/Images.lagda.md:303 and :311,
   and the private `rightAt` at :386, plus the single already-sealed consumer
   `right-at-pair` at src/L/Rud/Step.lagda.md:735-739. Images is 51.0 s for
   341 code lines, 0.150 s/line, the second-worst per-line rate in the
   surviving trunk after Pairing (`/tmp/t86-profile.txt`, `/tmp/t86-codelines.tsv`);
   three 26 s-class sites plus one-time proof bodies plausibly account for

   (src/L/Rud/Step.lagda.md:735-739), which is false today: the discipline
   was applied to Step's consumer but not to Images' own three sites. This is
   the best remaining lever by expected value.
2. **The `F10-spec` / `F8-spec` concrete-argument invocation cluster in

## 4. What the retiring subtree does that the trunk does not

Read the two largest retiring files, L.Godel.Closure (3,490 code lines,
102.1 s, 0.029 s/line) and L.Godel.Levels (2,775 code lines, 60.3 s,
0.022 s/line; profile rows in `/tmp/t86-profile.txt`, line counts in
`/tmp/t86-codelines.tsv`). The concrete style differences:

1. **Zero `opaque`, zero `postulate`, zero `TERMINATING` in either file, yet
   they check at about 1/5 to 1/35 of the hot modules' per-line rate.**
   `rg "opaque"` returns nothing in either file. Sealing is not what makes
   them cheap.
2. **Everything lives in module-parameterized blocks at an abstract carrier:
   `module _ (A : V ℓ) where`** (src/L/Godel/Closure.lagda.md:135,
   src/L/Godel/Levels.lagda.md:133), with `κ : ⟪ A ⟫ → V ℓ` and a membership
   proof as the only contact with the carrier. Statements are at variable
   indices over that carrier: `valuesAllTuples : (n : ℕ) → values
   (allTuples A (suc n)) ≡ A` (Closure:195), memberships like
   `⟨ v ∈ values (allTuples A (suc n)) ⟩` (Closure:150, :183), `⟨ γ ∈ z ⟩`
   (Closure:159).
3. **The substs are along locally proved equalities at abstract carriers**
   (`allTuples-suc` at Closure:159 and :189, `lookup-spec` at Closure:314-316,
   `numeralV≡#` at Levels:300, :327), so no conversion ever normalizes a
   concrete presentation. That is the P-h / I-4 / I-5 discipline held to the
   letter: nothing re-normalizes because nothing concrete is ever named.

Contrast with the surviving trunk's hot modules: SquareLaw states memberships
at `⟪ ω ⟫`, `⟪ sucV (γp p) ⟫` and `⟪ # n ⟫` shapes (T86 report section 2);
Condensation sits at concrete tower positions; Bridge's pre-fix γ family paid
the tower re-normalization. In one sentence for a LESSONS entry: **statements
at abstract carriers and variable indices make sealing unnecessary; sealing
is the substitute the trunk reaches for where the statement discipline was
broken.** The retiring files are also the counterexample to "unsealed means
slow": the 43% of the tree in `opaque` files is not where the unspent time
lives (T86 section 2), and these two files show why.

## 5. Verdict: is the tail the floor?

**For the six-line-seal class, yes: the tail is the floor.** Reading the
surviving trunk finds no second Bridge. Every read lemma ends where its
consumers write, every tower is sealed with the union body as the intended
endpoint, and the concrete-position statements are either at variable
indices or inside the two recorded design chapters (HF, Finite). The
campaign's levers are exactly what the ledger already books: Bridge done
(767 s), the retirement (355.5 s), SquareLaw and Condensation (held). The
ordinary surviving tail of about 341-375 s contains no Bridge-shaped
definition.

Two hedges, stated plainly. First, the R-38 invocation class is a different
arithmetic: Images still pays the 25.7 s-class `right-spec` invocation at
three of its own sites, and the `F10-spec` concrete-arg sites are unmeasured.
Those are tens of seconds at most, not wall-class, but they are real and
cheaper than anything else left. Second, the freeze's exit arithmetic has a
hole (section 3): whether the two live dispatches clear 1,100 s depends on
which Bridge rate is used, and the recorded 1,037 does not reproduce. So the
tail is the floor for the seal campaign, and F1 and F2 must carry the exit
condition by themselves, but the exit condition itself needs its arithmetic
re-derived before it is trusted.

- **New rule proposed (R-39, awaiting the owner's ID):** the invoke-once
  discipline of R-38 counts the operation's own definition sites, not only

  (`_build/r5a-report.md:468-481`); the counterexample is

  ending at a tower body is innocent when every consumer writes against the
  body (Lset-compute, Sset-compute, rank-compute, col-compute, π-compute);
  audit by reading the consumers' written forms, not the lemma's body.
