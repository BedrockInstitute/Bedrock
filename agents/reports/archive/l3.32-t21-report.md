## 1. The D-10 verdict: which formulation can the tree carry

The classical theorem is true (Hessenberg; Jech Theorem 3.5), so the question
is not truth but formulation. Three candidate routes to the SAME bound, an

| **C. Devlin 6.6/6.7**: the global Godel bijection `G : On × On → On` with the `γ + ω` successor case | global ordinal addition (`G(0,β) = sup(G(0,ν)+ν+ν)`) and the `γ+ω` structure | strictly worse than A at the delivered shape: needs the same absent arithmetic plus a global class recursion |

Route B is the one the delivered material can carry. The successor case of the
square law disappears entirely: the reduction `α ↦ κ = |α|` lands on an initial
ordinal, initial ordinals are limit ordinals, and the Godel-order argument runs
only on the limit/initial case. That is why the probe's decisive miniature is the
limit/initial case, and why no successor-step machinery appears anywhere in it.

1. **Containment** (`fst∈sucmax`/`snd∈sucmax`, lines 125-137): if `p ≺ q` then
   the coordinates of `p` are members of `sucV (max q)`, from `max-spec` and the
   `≤₁` bookkeeping. This is the order-theoretic fact that an initial segment
   below a pair with max `γ` sits inside `(γ+1)²`.
2. **The descent** (`colr≺`, `descent`, `g`, `g-inj`, lines 150-177): the
   truncation-dodging core. The hierarchy's union membership is truncated, so
   the pairing chapter's `col-img` witness (a member of a collapse is a collapse

   order with the hProp `col r ≡ b` extracts an honest witness, and the

   trichotomy cases refuted by `∈-irrefl`). The collapse is then equinumerous

3. **The chain** (`fiber²`, `h₀`, `h₀-inj`, lines 184-221): the initial segment

   law `ih` at `sucV γ` pushes it below `α`.
4. **The exclusion** (`exclude`, lines 226-274): `col p = α` would give, via the
   descent and the chain, an injection of `α` into `sucV (max p) ∈ α`, refuted
   by `no-inj-down`.
5. **Boundedness** (`gₚ`, `colp⊆α`, `col≤α`, `col∈α`, lines 262-322): the
   well-founded recursion on the Godel order. Each successor of a predecessor's
   collapse is still a member (`α-limit`) and the union of such members is a
   subset of `α` (transitivity); equality with `α` is excluded by step 4, so
   every collapse lies in `α`.
6. **The bound** (`τ⊆α`, `bound`, `bound-inj`, lines 324-370): `τ` is the union

   the fiber of that membership is the consumer's exact bound `⟪ τ ⟫ → ⟪ α ⟫`

   pairing chapter's `Pairing.bound`/`Pairing.bound-inj` shape, inlined.

## 3. What it assumed and why (what was not done)

Three module hypotheses, each priced, none measured:

- **`α-limit`**: initial ordinals are limit ordinals. Delivered by the
  initial-ordinal layer via the shift lemma (`γ+1 ≅ γ` for infinite `γ`) plus
  the definition of an initial ordinal. This is exactly what absorbs the
  successor case.
- **`ih`**: the square law at every `β ∈ α` in injection form. This is the
  `∈`-induction's hypothesis, discharged at `β < α` in the full chapter; it
  includes the finite/`ω` base (finite squares inject, finite well-orders have
  finite order types), a real but small piece that partially overlaps the
  initial-ordinal layer because finite ordinals are initial.
- **`no-inj-down`**: `α` injects into no member. This is initial-ordinal
  content: from `|α| = α` (initial) and injection ⟹ cardinal inequality.

Not done, and why: the `∈`-induction wrapper (routine over the delivered IH), the
transfer from `κ = |α|` back to `α` (straight bijection plumbing `⟪ α ⟫² ≅
⟪ κ ⟫²` via `α ≅ κ`, plus the least-of based inverse of `col→τ`, which is the
same measured descent pattern), and the finite/`ω` base (elementary counting,
inside T16's own allocation). One genuinely unmeasured risk inside the
initial-ordinal layer is flagged: extracting the equinumerosity witness
`α ≅ |α|` from the truncated least-of. The layer must produce it
non-truncatedly (an `∈`-induction carrying the bijection) or via
Schroeder-Bernstein (deferred by T18). This sits inside the 80-130 survey band
and may push it by 0-50 lines.
