## 1. Verdict

surviving masters at HEAD, the retirement set is 51 files and 30,648 lines,

The tree is not tight. The measured answer to the owner's question is that
roughly 700 to 850 lines of standing are written twice (the LevelFormula/
LevelSigma kit, the SquareLaw order core, the pair substrate, the Describe
shells, and the condensation level story), and that the single largest and
safest lever is the LevelFormula/LevelSigma mirror: 265 of LevelFormula's 613
code lines are byte-identical to lines in LevelSigma, and 388 of LevelSigma's
788 code lines match a LevelFormula line at similarity 0.85 or better.

Total net compression available from the anchored levers (A, C, D, E, F
below): about 520-720 lines naive, 680-940 calibrated (x1.3). One further
candidate (B, the condensation level story) is priced only at x3 and needs a
gate before it can be called a lever. Recorded, not argued from (D26): this
is between 2.9 and 4.0 percent of standing at the naive band.

**The single lever to do first is A**: extract the level-story clause kit that
LevelFormula and LevelSigma each state, byte-identical, into one module
parameterized by a transitive carrier, and instantiate it at both towers. It
is the largest net (about 230-280 naive), the lowest risk (no mathematical
content changes; the two consumers are `Everything` and `L.Rud.HF` only), and
it is exactly the P-h law applied to the survivors.

## 3. Method 1: rewrite-from-scratch findings

### 3.1 A. The LevelFormula/LevelSigma mirror (the first lever)

**The lines.** `src/L/LevelFormula.lagda.md` is 613 code lines and
`src/L/Rud/LevelSigma.lagda.md` is 788. Both instantiate the fresh generic
face (`L.InitialSegment`, imported at LevelFormula:52 and LevelSigma:53) at
their own tower, and both write the same clause kit from scratch. Measured by
exact line match, 265 of LevelFormula's code lines appear verbatim in
LevelSigma:

| Content | LevelFormula | LevelSigma | lines |
|---|---|---|---|
| pairForm, singleForm, zeroForm | 190-210 | 154-174 | 18 |
| Cl, Rg, the pair∈ decode | 267-341 | 181-243 | 32 |
| pairhood-out/in, single-out/in | 366-420 | 268-322 | 52 |

| module U/PK/PM opening, pairhood, singleValued | 121-135 | 115-129 | 12 |

The table lists the principal runs; the full 265 includes about 90 further
code lines in smaller runs (6, 6, 5 and 4-line blocks plus scattered exact
matches such as the zero-out tail at LevelFormula:429-435 versus
LevelSigma:331-337).

With the near-identical remainder (the range reads r-out/r-in at LevelFormula
817-850 versus LevelSigma 437-470 differ only in the local name `step` vs
`uStep`; the approximation reassembly ap-out/ap-in/a-ok at LevelFormula
751-767 versus LevelSigma 365-379; isOrd-out/in at LevelFormula 453-491 versus
LevelSigma 862-900), 388 of LevelSigma's 788 code lines match a LevelFormula
line at similarity 0.85 or better: 49 percent of the rud carrier's chapter is
a re-statement of the real tower's chapter. LevelSigma's own comment says it
out loud: "The ordinal predicate decodes to being an ordinal (the tower
chapter's shared decode, ported by carrier substitution)" at LevelSigma:860-861.
This is the T69/T70 mirror pattern the brief names, and the exact failure
P-h was written about.

**What replaces it.** One module, parameterized by `(u : V ℓ) (utr : isTransV u)`
plus the `DefOf u` inner world, hosting once: the pair∈ decode, the pairhood,
single-valuedness, zero-clause and ordinal decodes, the exact-domain clause
and its two-way decode, and the range read. The code is already carrier-generic:
none of the shared lines mention `Lset` or `Jset`; they use only `utr`, the
`PairKit`/`PairMem` modules (themselves generic over a transitive carrier,
PairAtoms:80, 144), `IsOrd`, and the inner semantics. The two consumers then
keep only their carrier-specific halves: LevelFormula's limit clause
(limitForm 259-260, limit-out/in 641-747) and its Def collapse (798-815);
LevelSigma's strengthened successor-value machinery (561-940) and its Sset
collapse (413-435).

**Price from the rewrite side.** The replacement is the existing code moved
once: about 300 lines for the kit plus a parameterized header, and about 20
lines of module application and re-export in the two consumers. Class x1.3:
every line of the kit already exists in both files, so the rewrite is a
delivered comparable, not a survey guess. Naive 305-335 written; NET = removed
(one of the two 300-line copies, minus the parts that stay carrier-specific)
minus written (the header and glue) = about +230 to +280. Calibrated (x1.3):
+300 to +365.

**Risk.** No mathematical content changes, so nothing is re-proved. What
moves: the two files' module headers and import lists, and any consumer that
names their internals. The consumer surface is small: only `Everything` and
`L.Rud.HF` import either file (HF:52 takes `module LevelAt` from LevelSigma),
so the LevelAt module interfaces must survive the extraction. The order of
work matters: LevelSigma's strengthened story reuses isOrd-out/in and the
domain decodes (LevelSigma:862-940), so the kit must land before those are
re-pointed. The one subtlety is that LevelSigma also has a weaker per-component
domain form (domForm 640-643, dom-out/in 903-940) that LevelFormula lacks; that
stays LevelSigma-local.

### 3.2 B. The condensation level story, folded into the face (candidate, gated)

`src/L/Condensation.lagda.md` writes the level story a third time, in a third
encoding: parameter-free formulas over `⊥*` with hand-written Kuratowski atoms
(sgl, pair2, kpair, fstEqTo, fstLt, sndIn, snd∈Snd at 328-407), the five clause
forms (isOrdAt 409-414, pairForm 455-459, domForm 463-480, singleForm 485-490,
zeroForm 494-499, limIn/limOut 503-525, limitForm 527-531, Rg 534-538, Ap
541-546, Cl 548-552), and their Delta-0 witnesses, assembled as levelStory
(556-563). The block is 180 code lines (measured 324-562). LevelFormula's
clause formulas (194-272) and LevelSigma's (158-186) state the same content
over `⟪ u ⟫` with the PairAtoms kit instead.

The unification direction is Condensation's: a parameter-free story over
`⊥*` with Delta-0 witnesses embeds at any carrier via `mapFo`/`embed`
(Condensation itself does this at σM 589-590 and σL 644-645), so it can be
the single source, with LevelFormula and LevelSigma embedding it and re-hosting
their decodes. But the decodes are the widest term: the pairhood/single/zero
decodes currently walk satisfaction of the `prAt` atoms (LevelFormula
313-450), and after embedding they must walk satisfaction of the embedded
formula instead. Describe's `Chain` (Describe:277-318) and the Delta-0
witnesses are delivered comparables that would anchor that walk, but the
re-proof itself is not in the tree anywhere.

**Price.** Class x3, and honestly not a priced lever yet: naive +100 to +250
after the kit from A exists, calibrated +300 to +750, with a real chance the
net is not positive if the decode re-proof runs long. The D-1 gate to run
before funding: one decode (say pairhood-out) against the embedded parameter-free
pairForm, stop-line about 80 lines. Until that probe is green, B is a
candidate, not a lever.

### 4.2 E. The pair substrate, hosted once

The singleton/pair/Kuratowski-pair characterization is written twice at the
meta level: `src/L/CardinalPredicates.lagda.md:152-233` (SglOf, PairOf,
sgl-char, pair-char, sglOf-subst, pairOf-subst, opr-char-fwd, inQ; 73 code
lines, measured) and `src/L/Coding/Base.lagda.md:196-262` (SglOf, PairOf,
sglOf-subst, pairOf-subst, prChar-fwd, prChar-bwd; 58 code lines, inside a
`private` block, plus the public sgl-char/pair-char at 149-175). The
statements are the same fact: a set is the singleton `{U}` exactly when it has
U as its only member, and is the pair `{U, W}` exactly when it has both and
nothing else; opr-char-fwd (CardinalPredicates:199) is prChar-fwd
(Coding/Base:221) under another name. CardinalPredicates does not import
Coding.Base at all, and Coding.Base keeps the machinery `private`, which is
exactly the C-14 law B failure: a private helper a consumer's proof needs is
re-derived instead of imported.

**What replaces it.** Host the characterization once (public, in Coding.Base,
which Describe already consumes for prChar-fwd/prChar-bwd at Describe:58-59),
import it in CardinalPredicates, and delete the private copies. Net: about
+80 to +100, class x1.3. Risk: low-medium; the two copies differ in variable
names and in the membership notation (`⟨ U ∈ w ⟩` vs `⟨ u ∈ x ⟩`), so one
canonical form must be chosen and the certificate uses re-pointed
(CardinalPredicates:251 and 368-376 in Coding.Base). CardinalPredicates' only
standing consumers are `Everything` plus the retiring Godel/Coding cone, which
keeps the blast radius small.

## 6. Method

All counts are non-blank lines inside Agda code fences, at HEAD, matching the
ledger basis (dev/LEDGER.md; scripts/ledger.py). Standing, the retirement set,
and the composition table were recomputed from the tree with the ledger's own
counting rule (regex over `git show HEAD:file`), not copied from prose; the
brief's table matched to the line. Similarity between files was measured with
`difflib.SequenceMatcher` over the code-line sequences, exact-match blocks
first, then a per-line similarity of 0.85 or better on matching 24-character
prefixes. No Agda was run, no files outside this report were written, and no
git state was touched.
