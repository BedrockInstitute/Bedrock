# [L3.32-T43] Where the counting calls the square law: the wall is on the critical path, at every tower stage and at every cardinal beyond `ω`

**Verdict in one paragraph.** The counting does not dodge T31's wall, and the
brief's conditional is resolved in the sharper direction: the GCH argument can
be *arranged* to call the square law only at initial ordinals, but that does
not put the wall off the critical path, because T31's law is itself not
delivered at any initial ordinal beyond `ω`. Devlin's route runs the tower
induction `|L_α| = |α|` over **all** ordinals `α ≥ ω`
(`_build/literature/dev2.txt:200`), and both of its cases multiply at the
induction variable: the limit case at every limit `λ`
(`dev2.txt:203-205`) and the successor case's formula count at every `α`
(`dev2.txt:216-221`). Corollary 5.4 then counts at an arbitrary parameter set
`X ⊆ L_α` (`dev2.txt:1360-1361`), 5.5 consumes the tower equality at
arbitrary `α, γ < κ` (`dev2.txt:1379-1382`), and 5.6 consumes it at `κ+`
(`dev2.txt:1387`). Classically every site reduces to the law at an initial
ordinal (`|α|`, `|X|`, `κ+`), but that reduction is exactly the T31 transfer
that the least-of truncation blocks (`_build/l3.32-t31-report.md`, §3), and
the law at the initial ordinals themselves, "everything beyond `ω` on the
initial-ordinal side" (T31 §3), is named, not delivered. Two observations keep
the verdict honest rather than merely bad. First, every conclusion-side
consumer of the counting is proposition-valued (the internal GCH sentence,
the `γ < κ` step, `|P(κ)| ≤ κ+`), so restating the bounds in truncated form
absorbs the extraction-risk truncation for free: the least-of output
`∥ ⟪ α ⟫ ≃ ⟪ κ ⟫ ∥₁` is delivered honestly (`src/L/Ordinal/SquareLaw.lagda.md:574-576`),
and the honest `α ≅ κ` that T31 cannot build is not needed by any conclusion.
Second, even so, the truncated square law is still needed at every cardinal
`≥ ω` (5.6's `κ+` alone forces it), and T31 delivers only `ω` and its finite
successors. **Verdict: YES, T31's obstruction is on the critical path to
`L ⊨ GCH`, and the cheapest next action is to deliver the square law at
initial ordinals in truncated form by the `∈`-induction (index-level
no-injection-down, truncated `ih`), not to attack the extraction.**

**Why the classical reading "at initial ordinals" does not rescue the formal
route.** Devlin's "easily seen" count (`dev2.txt:217`) and 5.4's "clearly"
(`dev2.txt:1360`) are reductions to the *cardinalities* `|L_α|`, `|X|`,
which are initial. The formal route cannot make that reduction without a
bijection `L_α ≃ |α|` (or `X ≃ |X|`): that is exactly T31's transfer, whose
least-of witness is the truncation `∥ ⟪ α ⟫ ≃ ⟪ κ ⟫ ∥₁` with no canonical
untruncated bijection to reconstruct (T31 §3; e.g. `⟪ ω ⟫ ≃ ⟪ ω + 1 ⟫`
admits the shift and many others). So at every site, the formal obligation is
either the law at a **non-initial** ordinal (sites 1, 2, 4 at `ω+ω`, `ω·ω`,
`γ`, ...), or the law at an initial ordinal beyond `ω` after a reduction
that is itself the wall.

## 2. Proposition-valuedness at the call sites (Q2)

The question is whether the truncated form of equinumerosity is enough.
Per site, the *consumer* of the product's output, and whether that
consumer's goal is proposition-valued:

| Site | What consumes the product's output | Goal proposition-valued? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (limit case at `λ`) | the tower equality `|L_λ| = |λ|`, consumed by 5.5 at `α, γ` and by 5.6 at `κ+` | **yes**: 5.5's conclusion `γ < κ` is a proposition, and `∥ γ ≃ α ∥₁` with `α < κ` eliminates into it (if `γ ≥ κ`, then `κ ↪ γ ≃ α` injects `κ` into `α < κ`, contradicting `κ` initial); 5.6's `|P(κ)| ≤ κ+` is a proposition, and the internal sentence `L ⊨ GCH` is proposition-valued satisfaction |
| 2 (successor case at `α`) | the tower equality at `α+1`, same downstream consumers | **yes** (same elimination) |
| 3 (5.4 at `X`) | `|M| = |L_α|` inside 5.5's chain, then `γ < κ` | **yes**: `∥ M ≃ L_α ∥₁` composed with the collapse `M ≅ L_γ` gives `∥ γ ≃ α ∥₁`, and the `γ < κ` step is the propositional argument above |
| 5 (5.6 at `κ+`) | `|P(κ)| = κ+` as the internal sentence's satisfaction | **yes** by construction: the internal predicates are already truncated, `HostEq a b = ∥ Σ[ f ] HostBij f a b ∥₁` (`src/L/CardinalPredicates.lagda.md:362-363`), and the adequacy certificates land in propositions |

The only function-valued consumers are the count's own internal compositions:
T1∘T2∘T17 as an actual injection inside the tower induction step and inside
5.4's bound construction (`_build/l3.32-t9-report.md`, §4). Those can be
restated truncated (`∥ sq α ∥₁` instead of a delivered `pair`), at the cost
that the tower induction's output becomes a truncated bound; the downstream
consumers of the table all absorb that, since their goals are propositions.
So the extraction-risk truncation is absorbable at every place that matters
for the GCH conclusion. The converse point matters more: absorbing the
truncation does **not** remove the law from the path, because the *proof* of
`∥ sq κ ∥₁` at the cardinals `κ` the counting reaches is still owed, and
T31 does not owe it.

## 3. The non-initial sites and the cost of the alternatives (Q3)

**The precisely named non-initial call sites.** In the tree's own route as
priced (T9/T31), the successor step calls T2 at the stage ordinal `α`
(`_build/l3.32-t9-report.md`, §4), and the limit step calls it at `λ`
(T18's table, the 1.1(vii) limit row, `_build/l3.32-t18-report.md` §2).
Both are the tower induction's variable, which ranges over all ordinals
`≥ ω` (dev2.txt:200), so `α = λ = ω+ω`, `ω·ω`, and every later
non-initial limit is a call site where T31's law is **not** delivered (T31
delivers `ω` and the successors of delivered ordinals, i.e. the finite
successors of `ω`, per T31 §3-§4). 5.5 then forces the tower equality at
arbitrary `α, γ < κ` (dev2.txt:1379-1382), which are non-initial in general.

**The alternatives, and what each costs:**

1. **A choice principle from `src/Base/Choice.lagda.md`.** It does not fix
   the extraction. `SetChoice` hands back a *truncated* choice function,
   `((x : X) → ∥ B x ∥₁) → ∥ ((x : X) → B x) ∥₁`
   (`src/Base/Choice.lagda.md:54-56`), and eliminating its output into
   `A ≃ B`, a non-proposition, is impossible, so it cannot manufacture the
   honest `α ≅ κ` from `∥ α ≃ κ ∥₁`. It is also strictly stronger than LEM
   (`choice→lem`, `src/Base/Choice.lagda.md:285-286`), so threading it
   through the counting chapters would make the tree's classical boundary
   more than the LEM parameter that `dev/PLAN.md:57` names as the
   postulate-free claim ("LEM is a module parameter, not a postulate");
   today `SetChoice` is spent only by `V.Model` (`V⊨ZFC`,
   `src/V/Model.lagda.md`). Cost without benefit: the honest transfer stays
   blocked, and the counting path's classical input becomes strictly
   stronger.
2. **A restatement of the bound in truncated form.** This is coherent and
   cheap on the consumer side: every conclusion site of §2 is
   proposition-valued, and the truncated transfer `∥ α ≃ |α| ∥₁` is
   *delivered* by the least-of search (`Card.least`'s witness,
   `src/L/Ordinal/SquareLaw.lagda.md:574-576`), so `∥ sq α ∥₁` follows from
   `∥ sq |α| ∥₁` propositionally. Its cost: the whole cardinality layer
   becomes proposition-shaped (no actual injection objects to compose), and
   the wall moves, it does not fall: the tower induction then needs
   `∥ sq κ ∥₁` at every cardinal `κ ≥ ω` reached (site 5's `κ+` alone), and
   T31 has not delivered the law at any cardinal beyond `ω`.
3. **A different route to the counting.** There is none that avoids the
   product. The count's upper bound contains the square law: the honest
   syntax injection `(a,b) ↦ (v₀ ∈̇ a) ∧̇ (v₀ ∈̇ b)` maps `⟪ X ⟫ × ⟪ X ⟫`
   into `Formula ⟪ X ⟫ 1` (`src/FOL/Syntax.lagda.md:94-105`), so
   `|X|² ↪ Formula ⟪ X ⟫ 1 ↪ max(|X|,ω)` would *be* the square law at
   `|X|`. Re-parameterizing 5.5's hull with `X = α ∪ {x}` instead of
   `L_α ∪ {x}` would remove the tower equality at `α` from the chain, but it
   breaks the transitive-fixing clause of condensation (`α ∪ {x}` is not
   transitive in general; Devlin's `L_α ∪ {x}` is chosen precisely because
   `L_α` is transitive and `x ⊆ L_α`, dev2.txt:1378-1379), and it still
   needs 5.4's count at a set of cardinality `|α|`. Neither is an honest
   classical shortcut.

## 4. The verdict, and the cheapest next action (Q4)

**Verdict: YES, the obstruction is on the critical path.** Two independent
reasons:

1. **5.6 alone forces a call at `κ+`.** The conclusion `|P(κ)| = κ+` needs
   `|L_{κ+}| = |κ+|` (dev2.txt:1387), i.e. the tower induction at the
   successor cardinal `κ+`, whose limit step multiplies at `κ+` and whose
   successor steps multiply at every ordinal below it. T31 delivers the law
   only at `ω` and the finite successors of `ω`; `κ+` is an initial ordinal
   beyond `ω`, in the exact set T31 names as undelivered ("the square law at
   the non-initial limit ordinals (and at every initial ordinal whose
   members contain one, so everything beyond `ω` on the initial-ordinal
   side)", T31 §3).
2. **The tower induction cannot be restricted to initial ordinals.**
   Devlin's induction runs over all `α ≥ ω` (dev2.txt:200), and 5.5
   consumes the equality at arbitrary `α, γ < κ` (dev2.txt:1379-1382). The
   classical reduction of an arbitrary site to its cardinal is the T31
   transfer; the truncated reduction is free, but it lands on the cardinals,
   where the law is still owed.

The brief's conditional is therefore resolved adversarially: the counting
*could* be arranged to call the law only at initial ordinals (truncated
form), but the wall is still on the critical path, because the set "where
the law fails" includes every initial ordinal beyond `ω`.

**Cheapest next action.** Attack the wall in the form the counting actually
consumes, which is smaller than the extraction:

- Restate the counting's bounds truncated (site by site from §2, all
  consumer goals are propositions). This is free on the consumer side and
  removes the extraction risk from the path: the truncated transfer is the
  delivered least-of witness, and the Core's `ih` hypothesis can itself be
  consumed truncated, since every elimination of it inside the Core lands in
  `Empty`, a proposition (the `exclude` case at
  `src/L/Ordinal/SquareLaw.lagda.md:418-436`), leaving the Core's honest
  `bound`/`bound-inj` intact.
- Then deliver the truncated square law at initial ordinals `≥ ω` by the
  `∈`-induction over ordinals that T21 priced as routine ("the
  `∈`-induction wrapper (routine over the delivered IH)",
  `_build/l3.32-t21-report.md` §3), discharging the Core's hypotheses at
  each cardinal: `ih` at infinite members by the induction hypothesis plus
  the truncated transfer, no-injection-down by initial-ness restated at the
  index level (T31 §5's own recorded cure: "Kept the cardinal's content at
  the index level (`Card.κ-min` over `⟪ sucV α ⟫`)"), the finite exclusion
  already delivered, and the base at `ω` already delivered (`CoreAtω`).
- Do **not** fund the extraction first: the honest `α ≅ κ` is blocked by
  T31's argument (no canonical bijection, CSB needs the missing injection)
  and is not on the cheapest path once the bounds are truncated.

This is a bounded build, not a free lunch: it is T21's "initial-ordinal
layer" (80-130 survey) plus the wrapper/transfer plumbing (50-90, anchored
by the measured descent pattern), with the measured Core as the anchor, and
with the index-level no-injection-down as the one piece T31 left
presentation-blocked but named.
