
## 2. The false step's exact role

The first-limit `carve-⊇` reads a member `y` of the family as a witness
`f ∈ Sset ω` with a pair `pr a y ∈ f` and an exact domain `δ ∈ Sset ω`
(`HF:547-556`). The load-bearing steps are:

- `δ ∈ ω` by `ord∈HF→∈ω δ ordδ δ∈u` (`HF:556`);
- `a ∈ ω` by ordinal transitivity (`HF:557-558`);
- `y ≡ Lset a` by `chainValue` (`HF:558`).

`ord∈HF→∈ω` is the carrier's ordinal content at the FIRST limit
(`HF:224-225`). It is proved by finiteness and tally (`HF:100-224`).

At a general limit, the bridge `a ∈ U l → a < l` is FALSE. The
machine-checked witness is `ω ∈ U ω ∖ ω` (`ProbeT194.agda:148-163`). The
exact domain of a witness is a member of `Sset (U l)`. Its ordinal content
bounds it below `U l`, not below `l`.

The proof needs the conclusion `a < l`. It does not need the bridge in its
full form. A true relativized form exists: the family formula itself can
carry the bound. If the formula says `a ∈ l`, then `carve-⊇` needs only the
chain `y ≡ Lset a`. The chain is new content, but the false bridge never
enters it.

The brief's guess, that the family's members all lie below `l` even though
`U l` holds more, is classically true for the natural formula. A witness
whose chain reaches a domain `δ₀ ≥ l` has rank above `U l`. It is not a
carrier member. But the tree has no rank machinery. A membership-style
proof bounds the domain only by `U l`. So the natural formula is a
hypothesis, not a finding. The explicit bound in the formula removes the
question.

### Candidate B: the natural formula with a rank argument

The formula stays the unbounded six-clause story. `carve-⊇` derives
`δ₀ < l` from the witness chain's rank. The chain at `δ₀` is a carrier
member only when `δ₀ < l`.

This is classically true. No delivered rank machinery exists. The
membership-style proof reduces to the same ordinal-content term. The
argument is a hypothesis, not a finding. It removes no term. Not
recommended.

### Candidate C: the carried sequence's own domain as the bound

The formula adds: the exact domain `δ₀` of `f` satisfies `δ₀ ∈ l`. The
domain is read through `exactDomForm` (`LevelKit:184`; decodes `:410`,
`:447`).

`carve-⊇` then uses ordinal transitivity: `a ∈ δ₀` and `δ₀ ∈ l` give
`a ∈ l`. `carve-⊆` needs chains at every `δ < l`. The carried sequence
supplies them.

Risk: the domain read adds formula and decode weight beyond candidate A's
pair-index bound. The bound still needs `l` as a parameter. Strictly more
expensive. Not recommended.

### Candidate D: the story's limit clause read as the bound

The limit clause forces the value at a limit index to the union of the
values below. It does not bound the index below `l`. It is a necessary
clause, not a bound. The union closure through the top value is the
`chainLimit` route (`BelowLim:346`). It does not remove `carve-⊇`.
Not a formulation on its own.
