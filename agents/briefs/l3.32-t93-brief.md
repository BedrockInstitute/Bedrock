## THE DEFECT, EXACTLY, BECAUSE IT IS DETECTABLE BY READING

`[L3.32-T86]` profiled `L.Rud.Bridge` at 939 seconds and found **95 percent of
it in one family**. The cause:

- A read lemma, `γ-compute`, had a stated type that **ended one layer too
  early**: it stopped at the tower body rather than at the form its consumers
  actually use.
- So each of about ten `subst` consumers **re-normalized the union tower inside
  `towerStep`**, paying the same conversion roughly eight times.
- The fix was **six lines**: restate the lemma to end where consumers use it,
  and absorb the normalization once inside an `opaque` seal. Measured 939 s to
  199 s, reproduced independently.

See the delivered fix at `src/L/Rud/Bridge.lagda.md`, the `towerStep≡+ωU` and
`γ-compute` pair, and read `_build/l3.32-t86-report.md` sections 2 and 4 for the
full anatomy. **That fix is your template and your detector.**

## THE SHAPE YOU ARE HUNTING, STATED AS A CHECKLIST

For each module, look for a definition where ALL of these hold:

1. It is a **read lemma or a computation lemma**: something whose job is to let
   a consumer rewrite, typically consumed through `subst`, `transport`, `cong`,
   or a `rewrite`.
2. Its **stated right-hand side is not the form its consumers use.** This is
   the crux. Find the lemma, then grep its consumers, and ask: does every
   consumer immediately have to normalize further to get what it wanted?
3. It has **several consumers**, so the cost is paid repeatedly. One consumer
   is not a Bridge; ten is.
4. The intermediate form it stops at is **expensive to normalize**: a union, a
   tower step, a fixpoint unfolding, a large record.

A definition satisfying 1 to 4 is a candidate. Rank by (number of consumers) x
(how expensive the skipped normalization looks).
