## THE THREE QUESTIONS, and the third is the delicate one

**1. Which files can take a `gch_assign` declaration today?**
The mechanism has two safeties and you must respect both: a declaration
is legal only for a master in NEITHER closure, and each must name its
wing consumer. A dead declaration is a checker defect, not a saving.
Two exist already (`src/FOL/Count.lagda.md`, `src/V/Collapse.lagda.md`).
Find every other master that qualifies. For each, name the wing
consumer at `file:line` and show that no AC-side master imports it.
**C-23 binds**: a cross-file scan alone lies about same-file
instantiation bodies.

**2. Where does the closure boundary actually fall INSIDE the shared
files?** `[T155]` did this at definition granularity and its report is
your starting point, not your answer: the tree has changed a great deal
since, including today's `L.LevelKit`, `L.Rud.StepStory`,
`L.Rud.LevelSigma` and `L.Rud.Bridge`. Re-measure. Report, per shared
master, how many lines are AC-reachable and how many are GCH-only.

**3. Is there a NATURAL split that happens to follow that boundary?**

**This is the question, and its phrasing is the whole task.** D36's
condition, in the owner's words, forbids exactly the thing that would
be easiest here:

> the FINAL product's module divisions must stand on their own
> architectural and pedagogical logic, never contorted to pass the
> threshold

**So do NOT ask "how do we split these files to move lines out of the
meter".** That is the contortion the condition names, and a split
designed to move a number will be found by the naturalness review at
the landing and will have to be undone.

**Ask instead: does this file hold two subjects?** A file whose
AC-reachable half and GCH-only half are ALSO two different subjects is
a file that should have been split on its own merits, and the closure
boundary merely reveals it. `[T252]` found exactly this shape in
`L.Rud.StepStory` today, by subject alone and with no reference to
closures: the file announced one subject and held two.

For each candidate, answer in this order and stop at the first no:
- Does the file hold two subjects a reader would name separately?
- Would you split it if the closure boundary did not exist?
- Does the split line coincide with the closure boundary?

**Only a file that passes all three is a proposal.** A file that passes
the third alone is a contortion, and reporting it as one is a finding.

## WHAT I EXPECT, so you can refute it

My own reading, stated so you can test it rather than inherit it: I
expect FEW files to pass all three. Shared content is shared because
both wings use it, which usually means it is one subject serving two
consumers rather than two subjects sharing a file. If that is right,
the honest answer is that most of the 2,100-2,700 is reachable only by
the deletion test's own reading and not by splitting, and the campaign
should stop trying to move it in the meter.

**A finding of "almost none of it splits naturally" is a full result**
and it closes the owner's question with evidence.
