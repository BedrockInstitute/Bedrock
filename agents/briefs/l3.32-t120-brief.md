## THE SITUATION

You have just joined this repository. **You know nothing about it.** This brief
deliberately tells you nothing about the project, because you ARE the
experiment: the question is how far the repository's own documents carry a new
agent.

**Start at `AGENTS.md` in the repository root.** Follow wherever it points you,
as far as you judge useful. Then report what you learned, what you could not
learn, and what cost you time.

Do not read this brief for hints. It has none on purpose.

## WHAT TO REPORT

1. **What is this project building, and how?** Answer as if explaining to
   another new agent. Then say how confident you are and what you inferred
   rather than read.
2. **How is the work actually run?** Who does what, how a change gets made,
   what must happen before it lands.
3. **What rules and disciplines bind you**, and where did they come from? If
   the documents explain WHY a rule exists, say which ones do and which are
   bare assertions.
4. **What could you NOT determine**, and what did you have to guess at? Be
   specific: name the question and where you looked for it.
5. **What took the longest to find**, and what path would have been faster?
6. **State three things you believe confidently and might be WRONG about.**
   The orchestrator will check them. This is the most useful section in the
   report: a confident wrong belief formed from these documents is a defect in
   the documents, and it is invisible to anyone who already knows the answer.
7. **If you had to start real work in this repository tomorrow, what is the one
   document change that would help you most?**

## HOW TO WORK

Read, do not build. Do not run Agda, do not run `make check`, do not write to
`src/` or `dev/`, do not use git beyond reading. You may run read-only commands
freely.

**Timebox your reading and say where you stopped.** If you run out of budget
before finishing, an honest partial map with "I never opened X" is worth more
than a confident summary of documents you skimmed.
