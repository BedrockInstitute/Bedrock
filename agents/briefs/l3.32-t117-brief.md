## THE MERGE, WHICH IS THE REAL DESIGN PROBLEM

The two panels overlap because **they are two views of one thing**: the route
is the decision procedure, and the workbench is where the orchestrator
currently stands inside it. A reader wants one picture that answers *where are
we, what happens next, and what would change it.*

So the strong move is one panel: **the route graph, with the current position
marked on it**, and the workbench's four sections attached to the nodes they
belong to rather than listed separately. The workbench's own structure already
maps onto the graph:

- `DISPATCHED NOW` is a position marker.
- `QUEUED, AND WHAT RELEASES EACH` is an edge with a condition on it, which is
  what the route graph already draws.
- `CONDITIONS THAT WOULD CHANGE WHAT I DISPATCH` is a branch.
- `NOT DISPATCHING, AND WHY` is a set of nodes already in the lever branch.

**Test that reading before building on it.** If the two genuinely do not merge,
say so and arrange them adjacently instead; a forced merge that loses
information is worse than two honest panels.

## THE CHARTS

Pie charts for the line distribution and the seconds distribution. Data:

- lines: `dev/ledger.toml`'s `[[remaining]]` rows carry the bands, and
  `python3 scripts/ledger.py --brief` gives standing measured from HEAD.
- seconds: `[timing].full_cold_seconds`, the `[[hot]]` module rows, and the
  `[[tree_cost]]` subtree rows.

**Constraints on the charts.**

- **SVG generated in Python, inline in the page.** No library, no script, no
  network. A pie slice is an arc path; this is arithmetic, not a dependency.
- **A pie with fifteen slices is a worse list than a list.** Group the tail,
  label what matters, and say what the grouped remainder is.
- **Label the total.** A distribution without its absolute is decoration: the
  reader needs 1,099 s and 18,464 lines, not only the proportions.
- If a chart would mislead (bands are ranges, not points; standing and
  remaining are different kinds of quantity), **say so in the panel** rather
  than drawing something tidy and wrong.
