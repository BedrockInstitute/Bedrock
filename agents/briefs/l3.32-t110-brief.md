## WHAT TO BUILD

**A maintenance mechanism, which is a checker plus a cadence, not a document
telling somebody to remember.** This project's own rule, in `AGENTS.md`: a rule
that is not machine-enforced must name its enforcement point, and a rule with
no enforcement point is a wish. **A maintenance mechanism whose enforcement
point is "the orchestrator, periodically" is exactly the thing that failed.**

Design it yourself, but it must answer:

1. **Which decay modes above are mechanically detectable?** For each, the check
   and its threshold. Size caps are the obvious ones and thresholds must be
   argued, not guessed: say what number you chose and why that number.
2. **Which are NOT mechanically detectable**, and what the honest enforcement
   point is for those. Saying "this one cannot be automated, here is the review
   step that catches it" is a real answer.
3. **What cadence.** Some checks belong in `make check` (cheap, every commit).
   Others are too slow or too noisy and belong in an on-demand sweep. **Put
   nothing expensive in the commit gate**: the gate has to stay cheap enough
   that people actually run it, and this project has spent the whole day
   reducing exactly that cost.
4. **How a threshold is raised.** A cap that anybody may raise silently is not
   a cap. Say who may and what they must record.

## THE TRAP THIS DISPATCH MUST AVOID

**Do not produce a checker that cries wolf.** A first version of
`check-rule-ids.py` today flagged `T-function`, `I-ideal`, `D-bucket` and
`C-prime`, which are ordinary English written with a capital, and its author
noted the obvious: a checker that fires on prose is a checker that gets
disabled. **Run yours against the real tree and tune it until its output is all
true positives**, then report the tuning you did.
