## WHAT TO DO

1. **Add a supersession marker to each superseded entry**, in one
   consistent form, appended to the entry and clearly separated from
   the original text. Propose the form in your report; something like a
   final sentence beginning `**SUPERSEDED** by `[Tnnn]`:` with the new
   figure and its report line. **Do not touch a single word of what is
   already there.**
2. **Document the two entry conventions in the header.** The early
   L3.32 era writes a DISPATCHED entry and a RETURNED entry for one
   task (`[T7]` at `:1023` and `:1118`, `[T16]` at `:1201` and `:1277`,
   `[T49]` at `:1696` and `:1705`). The later era writes one entry per
   task. Nothing says so, and a reader scanning for duplicate codes
   finds what look like defects. The orchestrator's own scan reported
   them as duplicates this morning, which is the evidence that the
   header needs this.
3. **Check the "Reading order" paragraph at `:14-19` is still true**
   after T246's insertions, and correct it if it is not.
4. **Seven entry paragraphs exceed 1,400 characters.** Split ONLY where
   a split adds no words and removes none: a paragraph break at a
   sentence boundary. If a split would require rewording, leave it and
   list it.

stop-line, an overage of 3,245, and said correctly that it is the

holding.** Recent entries keep the one-paragraph, three-to-six-sentence

So: **do not compress existing entries to hit a total.** Your job is
the opposite of compression. If you want to record the file's size, add
it as a tracked figure in the header, not as a limit.

## WHAT NOT TO DO

- **Do not reorder entries.** Chronology is the file's index.
- **Do not merge the dispatch and return pairs.** They are two events.
- **Do not delete anything**, including a claim a later entry refuted.
  The refuted claim is the record.
- **Do not add a summary, a table of contents or an index** unless your
  report argues for it first and the argument is about a reader's real
  need. This file has grown by accretion and the cheapest way to make
  it worse is to add a layer that goes stale.
