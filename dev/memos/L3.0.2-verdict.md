# [L3.0.2] Verdict on the big lever, and what `hasChoice` actually costs

Deliverable of goal `[L3.0.2]` (PLAN §6.1 phase C). Developer doc, English only. Consumes
`[L3.0.3]`'s probe, `[L3.0.4]`'s statement memo and its §7b supersession, and the
`L.Recursion` chapter delivered under `[L3.0.1]`.

Answers one question: **as the "big lever" of D12, how much of `hasChoice` does the
internalization theorem remove, and what does the 42k become?**

Short answer: the lever works, but it is **not the lever D12 named**, and the number is far
better than D12 projected. Sections 1 to 3 measure, §4 projects with stated confidence,
§5 is the ruling.

## 2. The empirical anchor: what Bedrock has actually delivered

Two chunks exist in both repositories, so the compression on them is measured, not
projected.

| Chunk | Source | Bedrock | Ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| reflection through arbitrary-φ separation and replacement | 2,283 | 721 | **3.2x** |
| coding substrate | 2,638 | 1,065 | **2.5x** |
| internalization harness (three instances) | 4,673 | 96 | **49x** |

The source chunk for the first row is `Reflect` + `ReflectN` + `ReflectFo` + `ModelACSep` +
`ModelACReduce` + `Stage`, **plus** `WellOrder` + `FormulaOrder` + `ConstructibleOrder`,
which the source's reflection drags in because it picks its Montague witness with `<L`.
Bedrock does not, so those 742 lines are not in its column at all.

**A caveat on that first ratio, which is a selected slice.** It is defensible for the
functionality it names, but it is not the ratio on everything Bedrock has built. Taken by
whole buckets, the two the book has covered go **5,212 → 3,409, which is 1.53x**:
reflection and assembly 3,517 → 2,344, coding substrate 1,695 → 1,065. The difference is
that the 3.2x slice is where Bedrock's design departs from the source, while the buckets
also contain straight ports (`Constructible`, `Definability`, `Ordinal`) where there is no
design advantage to take and the gain is ordinary proof hygiene.

So compression here is **strongly uneven**, and the three regimes should be named:

| Regime | Measured ratio |
|---|---:|
| straight port, no design difference | 1.2x to 1.6x |
| slice where the design departs | ~3x |
| pure Δ₀ tax (the internalization harness) | 49x |

The projection in §4 therefore turns entirely on **how much of the remaining 43k is tax
rather than mathematics**, and that is the estimate's real load-bearing judgement, not any
single measured ratio.

A second anchor for per-clause cost: `L.ReflectFo` is 268 lines and contains **four**
twelve-clause traversals over the formula constructors (`Answers`, `Answers-mono`,
`gstep`, `reflectFo`), which puts a twelve-clause traversal with its proofs at about
**65 lines** in Bedrock's idiom. That is the unit the projections below are built from.

## 3. The finding: D12 named the wrong lever

D12 ruled "take the big lever first", the lever being a general internalization theorem
for `L`-recursion, projected to reach about 65% of the certificate mass and to cost 1,400
to 2,000 lines itself.

The theorem is **96 lines**. It is a wrapper around `hasReplacementL`.

The reason the source needs 13,518 lines for tier 1 is that its comprehension fields are
**Δ₀-only**, so any recursively-defined table has to be made definable *inside a stage*,
where a formula does not mean what it means outside. That forces absoluteness, hence a Δ₀
certificate, hence per-clause Δ₀ and bounding witnesses, a bounding ordinal, a relabelling
layer, and a coherence argument for the approximation sequence. Every one of those exists
to pay the Δ₀ tax, and none of them is about recursion.

`[L2.2]` removed the tax: separation and replacement in `L` hold for formulas of **any**
complexity and are read at the class model. So the real lever was general-formula
comprehension, and it was paid one goal earlier than D12 expected, under a different code.
What `[L3.0]` contributes is the 96-line wrapper and the reframing that makes an
instance's obligation exactly one thing: a defining formula and its adequacy.

This matters for the projection because the buckets are **not** uniformly compressible.
The measured 2.5x to 3.2x came from chunks that were only moderately Δ₀-taxed. The three
large uncovered buckets are disproportionately Δ₀ tax, so they should do considerably
better, and §4 says by how much and on what basis.

## 4. What the 43k becomes

Bedrock's `src/L` currently stands at **3,361 lines** and covers the reflection, coding
and axiom buckets. The uncovered remainder is roughly **43,000 lines**, which is within
1.5% of the 42,354 that PLAN §2.1 attributed exclusively to `hasChoice`.

| Bucket | Source | Projected | Basis | Confidence |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| satisfaction | 16,400 | 800 to 1,600 | satisfaction is already a meta function (`FOL.Semantics`); what is owed is one `Definition`: a graph over twelve constructors plus soundness and completeness, three traversals at ~65 lines, plus frame and coded-syntax plumbing. `TarskiObj*` (3,214), `Coh*` (2,554) and `SatWitness*` (2,596) are Δ₀ certificate, coherence and stage-7 witness production respectively, and all three are removed outright | medium |
| recursion tables | 12,019 | 600 to 1,500 | `Depth*` is a **termination measure** for the comparison recursion; Agda's structural recursion needs none, so it is expected to vanish entirely. `Cmp` is a decidable comparison on `Formula`, a meta function of order 100 lines, plus its graph | medium-high |
| order and choice | 11,929 | 1,200 to 2,500 | the genuine mathematics of the book: that `<L` is a well-order, and choice from it. Meta-level in Agda, internalized by one `Definition`. `OrderGraphInL*` / `OrderInL` / `OrderAdequacy` are Δ₀ tax and go; `OrderSound` and the lex arguments largely survive as meta proofs | **medium-low** |
| other | 2,669 | 400 to 800 | pro rata at the measured 3.2x, less the Δ₀ share | low |
| **remaining total** | **43,017** | **3,000 to 6,400** | | |

So the answer to the headline question: **the 42k to 45k becomes roughly 3,000 to 6,400
lines**, and the whole `L` side of the book lands at about **6,500 to 10,000**, of which
3,361 is already written.

That is a **7x to 14x** compression, against D12's projected 3x.

**Where the projection is weakest.** The order-and-choice bucket, at medium-low
confidence, is the one with the least evidence behind it: it holds the only mathematics in
the cone that is not scaffolding, no part of it has been built in Bedrock, and its 11,929
lines contain a mixture of tax and content that the name-prefix classification cannot
separate. If the estimate is wrong, it is wrong there, and the honest error bar on that
row alone is a factor of two. The satisfaction row is better founded because its structure
is known clause by clause and the per-clause unit is measured.

**What would move the number.** Writing the satisfaction instance would settle the largest
bucket and the largest single uncertainty at once; it is the natural next unit and it is
what `[L3.0.1]`'s instance half should be re-pointed at, in preference to `Depth`, which
the same analysis expects to disappear.

## 5. Ruling

**Verdict: green**, with the reason restated. The route is not "a large theorem absorbs the
certificate mass"; it is "general-formula comprehension removes the reason the certificate
mass exists, and a 96-line wrapper turns what remains into a form an instance fills". The
rollout is unaffected: `[L3.5]` to `[L3.7]` proceed as instantiations of `L.Recursion`.

Consequent re-scopings, all recorded in PLAN §11:

- `[L3.11]` (clause bundle) stays **conditional**. With the graph unconstrained there is
  no twelve-way Δ₀ grid to collapse, and two of `ClauseBundle`'s five fields are already
  gone. Build it only if an instance shows it is wanted.
- `[L3.12]` and `[L3.13]` (tiers 2 and 3) were separated from tier 1 by the complexity
  boundary that has dissolved. **Re-examine before opening**; on present evidence both are
  ordinary instances of `L.Recursion` rather than separate theorems.
- `[L3.4]` (scaffolding parameterization, the red-verdict fallback) **does not open**.
- `[L3.2]` (`reify!`) remains off the critical path, as an opportunistic accelerator.

The one number that should not be quoted without its caveat is the 7x to 14x. It is a
projection from two measured ratios and one measured per-clause unit, not a measurement,
and §4 names the row that would break it.
